# MRI segmentation and surfacing of cortical laminae, and multi-echo GRE contrast mechanisms

Scope note: equivolume/Laplace frames (Waehnert 2014 [NeuroImage 93:210, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.03.078], Leprince, LayNii, Nighres), BigBrain (Wagstyl), Fracasso 2016, Zeng 2024 are covered elsewhere and are only referenced here in one line. Citation status: "verified" means title/authors/venue/year/DOI were confirmed through PubMed metadata in this session. "PMID only" means a citation match returned a PMID but I did not read the record. "Unverified" means not confirmed.

User protocol for reference: 7T 3D FLASH magnitude, 160 µm, TE 9.37/20.71/32.05 ms, TR 80 ms, flip 25°, fixed tissue in Fomblin.

## Q1. Which ex vivo / high-res methods segment or surface laminae (vs. only show contrast), how, at what resolution, how validated, what failed

### Takeaway
Almost all of the ex vivo laminar MRI literature *shows* laminar contrast. It validates that contrast qualitatively against histology and does not segment layers. The few methods that do segment layers fall into three groups. (1) Manual labels: Augustinack 2005, Fischl 2009. (2) Unsupervised voxel or profile clustering with **no spatial or ordering prior**: Barazany & Assaf 2012, Bastiani 2016. (3) Surface frames from GM/WM boundaries plus intermediate depth surfaces: Costantini 2023, Bazin 2014 / Nighres. These place depth surfaces by geometry, not by fitting laminar bands. No method found here fits laminar surfaces to the band contrast jointly with ordering, smoothness and continuity constraints. Where numbers exist, validation is coarse: χ² contingency agreement of about 0.92–0.93 against histology for 2–4 layer *complexes* at 340 µm (Bastiani), and MRI–histology boundary registration of 306 µm (75th percentile) and 527 µm (95th percentile) (Costantini). No study reports a per-layer surface error in µm against histology.

### Cited Findings

**Contrast-only ex vivo studies (no segmentation)**
- Fatterpekar et al. 2002, "Cytoarchitecture of the human cerebral cortex: MR microscopy of excised specimens at 9.4 Tesla", AJNR 23(8):1313–21 (verified, PMID 12223371). Method: formalin-fixed samples of calcarine, Heschl's, somatosensory, M1, frontal pole, hippocampus and retrosplenial cortex, scanned at 9.4T with intermediate-weighted sequences, about 14 h 17 min per specimen. Resolution was **78 × 78 × 500 µm**, which resolved horizontal lamination including the external band of Baillarger. Validation was intensity profiles across the cortex, compared with histology of the same sections. Key physics: the profiles were "greatest hypointensity at the sites of maximum myelin concentration and maximum cell density". In other words, the hypointensity has two causes (myelin and cell density) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12223371/)
- Augustinack et al. 2005, "Detection of entorhinal layer II using 7Tesla magnetic resonance imaging", Ann Neurol 57(4):489–94, doi 10.1002/ana.20426 (verified). Autopsy temporal lobe blocks were scanned at 7T with a solenoid coil at **70 and 100 µm isotropic**. Layer II islands were visible through the full anterior–posterior extent of EC, with "similar size and shape" in MRI and histology. Validation was qualitative, with no error metric — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.20426)
- Sengupta et al. 2018, "High resolution anatomical and quantitative MRI of the entire human occipital lobe ex vivo at 9.4T", NeuroImage 168:162–171, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.03.039 (verified). Whole occipital lobe, about 80 mm cube, with a 16-channel receive array and kT-points pTx. Anatomical T2*w GRE at 500 µm (TR/TE 36/17.68 ms), **100 µm (47/22 ms, 6.8 h)** and **60 µm (46/20.1 ms, 18 h)**. Multi-echo qT2* at **200 µm, TE 6.45/14.69/22.93/31.17 ms, TR 38.5 ms, flip 25°, 1.4 h**. This echo train is very close to the user's protocol. Results: the stria was visible at 100 and 60 µm, and the qT2* maps show a higher-T2* superficial band separated from the deep layers by the stria throughout V1. **No layer segmentation and no histological validation.** The V1/V2 border was followed qualitatively. Tissue was immersed in **Fluorinert FC-3283, not Fomblin**, after a 6-week PBS wash. WM T2* was about 20 ms and deep GM about 26 ms. T2* was higher superficially than in deep cortex — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5862655/)
- Edlow et al. 2019, "7 Tesla MRI of the ex vivo human brain at 100 micron resolution", Sci Data 6:244, doi 10.1038/s41597-019-0254-8 (verified). Whole brain, 10% formalin, 31-channel coil. Single-echo multi-flip FLASH at **100 µm isotropic**, with **100 h scan time (25 h per flip)**, and synthesized FLASH volumes. A data resource only, with no laminar segmentation — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-019-0254-8)
- Kleinnijenhuis et al. 2013, "Layer-specific diffusion weighted imaging in human primary visual cortex in vitro", Cortex 49(9):2569–82, doi 10.1016/j.cortex.2012.11.015 (verified). V1 at 11.7T DWI. FA varied with depth and was *reduced* in the stria of Gennari, the inner Baillarger band and the deepest layer. Contrast only — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2012.11.015)
- Leuze et al. 2014, "Layer-specific intracortical connectivity revealed with diffusion MRI", Cereb Cortex 24(2):328–39, doi 10.1093/cercor/bhs311 (verified). Post mortem dMRI analysed in **4 notional laminae**, which were depth bins, not segmented layers: tangential at the surface, radial below, mixed radial and tangential at the stria, radial deep — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhs311)
- Trampel et al. 2011, "Do the congenitally blind have a stria of Gennari? First intracortical insights in vivo", Cereb Cortex 21(9):2075–81, doi 10.1093/cercor/bhq282 (verified). In vivo 7T. The stria was detected reliably in sighted and congenitally blind subjects. Detection only — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhq282)
- Trampel et al. 2019, "In-vivo magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of laminae in the human cortex", NeuroImage 197:707–715, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.09.037 (verified). A review. It names spatial resolution, precision and specificity as the open challenges — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.09.037)
- Edwards et al. 2018, "Microstructural imaging of human neocortex in vivo", NeuroImage 182:184–206, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.02.055 (verified). A review: R1 is the myelin proxy for parcellation and R2* tracks iron with age. Multiple complementary qMRI maps increase specificity — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.02.055)

**Methods that label or segment**
- Fischl et al. 2009, "Predicting the location of entorhinal cortex from MRI", NeuroImage 47(1):8–17, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2009.04.033 (verified). 7T multi-echo FLASH at **100 µm**, TR 40 ms, flips 15/20/25°, TE 8/16/24/32 ms. The CNR-per-time optimum was **TR 40, TE 20, α 23°**. Fixed-tissue values: **T2* WM 11 ms, GM 26 ms**, T1 WM 770 ms, GM 1078 ms. The authors state that T2* differences are the dominant contrast source in fixed samples. EC borders were **manually** drawn by one neuroanatomist from layer II islands, lamina dissecans and the deep pyramidal layer, in 9 left and 8 right hemispheres. Labels were then sampled onto the surface and cleaned with **binary closing (3 dilations, 3 erosions)**. Validation against Nissl was qualitative. Cross-subject boundary variability was about **3.5 mm (left) and 2.5 mm (right)**, measured by a modified Hausdorff distance, and in vivo prediction was "within 3 mm". **Going from 100 to 120 µm made the anterior and medial EC borders hard or impossible to detect** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2738987/)
- Augustinack et al. 2014, "MRI parcellation of ex vivo medial temporal lobe", NeuroImage 93:252 (PMID 23702414 only; I did not read it).
- Barazany & Assaf 2012, "Visualization of cortical lamination patterns with magnetic resonance imaging", Cereb Cortex 22(9):2016–23, doi 10.1093/cercor/bhr277 (verified; PubMed lists it as 2011 e-pub). In vivo multi-inversion-recovery in humans and rats. "Corticograms" were cluster-analysed into **at least 6 laminar compartments**. Validation was a correspondence with histology, with the caveat that the two "do not represent similar quantities" — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhr277)
- Bastiani et al. 2016, "Automatic Segmentation of Human Cortical Layer-Complexes and Architectural Areas Using Diffusion MRI and Its Validation", Front Neurosci 10:487, doi 10.3389/fnins.2016.00487 (verified). One block of M1 and premotor cortex at 9.4T. Diffusion-prepared SE at **340 µm**, b 3000, 60 directions. Features: 31 per depth point (total, radial and tangential ADC plus 28 SH coefficients), sampled at 9 Laplace depths. **k-means with no spatial information.** Silhouette analysis gave k = 3 or 6, read as 2 or 4 layer clusters **plus noise clusters at tissue boundaries**. Areal borders were found by **Mahalanobis or Hotelling's T² between blocks of depth profiles (12–24 profiles, Schleicher approach)**. Two consistent borders were found: the precentral crown and the precentral sulcus fundus. Validation (Gallyas myelin and cresyl violet on 60 µm sections, contingency tables): myelo **0.93 mean** (≥0.87) and cyto **0.92** (≥0.83). Scan–rescan agreement was 0.97 for 2 clusters and 0.86 for 4 clusters (range 0.79–0.94). Failures: **layer 1 (100–200 µm) was sometimes missed (partial volume at 340 µm)**; the 4-cluster solution was less stable; Laplace depth has a curvature bias; there was only one specimen — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5102896/)
- Bazin et al. 2014, "A computational framework for ultra-high resolution cortical segmentation at 7 Tesla", NeuroImage 93 Pt 2:201–9, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.03.077 (verified). Multi-object, **topology-preserving** deformable models (MGDM), combined with shape and intensity atlases. This is a *tissue-class* segmentation (GM/WM/CSF/subcortical), not a laminar one. Validation was on simulated and real images — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.03.077)
- Costantini et al. 2023, "A cellular resolution atlas of Broca's area", Sci Adv 9(41):eadg3844, doi 10.1126/sciadv.adg3844 (verified). Whole hemisphere, 7T, 31-channel coil, ME-FLASH at **150 µm**, TE 5.65/11.95/18.25/24.55 ms, flips 10/20/30°. The reference image is the RMS of the echoes at 20°. B0, B1+ and B1− corrections were applied. **Only the infra/supragranular boundary is segmented in MRI**; the paper does not say whether this was manual or automatic. Cortex is surfaced with SAMSEG plus FreeSurfer: white, pial and **10 intermediate surfaces**. MRI layer boundaries are not validated against cell counts. MRI–histology boundary registration was **306 ± 96 µm (75th percentile) and 527 ± 137 µm (95th percentile)**, computed in 2D, so these are upper bounds — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10569704/)
- Semi-automated V1 delineation from laminar profiles at 7T: an ISMRM 2011 abstract clusters stria-bearing profiles and states that quantitative validation is still pending (authors not checked) — [ISMRM 2011 #2359](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/11MProceedings/PDFfiles/2359.pdf). ISMRM 2013 #267 does automated stria masking — [ISMRM 2013](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/13MProceedings/PDFfiles/0267.PDF). A 2005 Hum Brain Mapp study found that about **80% of the stria** could be visualized under optimal conditions, with large intersubject variability (authors unverified) — [PubMed 15954140](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15954140)
- A related direction (not verified in detail): a 2024 bioRxiv paper segments cortical cytoarchitectonic domains directly from ultra-high-resolution whole-brain dMRI — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.14.618245.full.pdf). A macaque MAP-MRI study at 200 µm clusters voxels from microstructure alone, with no spatial information. Its laminar patterns resemble histology and its layer transitions match histology better than atlas borders do — [PMC11507751](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11507751)

**Validation modalities (optical)**
- Magnain et al. 2014, "Blockface histology with optical coherence tomography: a comparison with Nissl staining", NeuroImage 84:524–33, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.08.072 (verified). SD-OCT on the block face. Two observers manually labeled layers III, IV and V in OCT and in Nissl and found "good agreement". OCT–blockface registration was significantly more accurate than Nissl–blockface registration — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.08.072)
- Wang H. et al. 2018, "as-PSOCT: Volumetric microscopic imaging of human brain architecture and connectivity", NeuroImage 165:56–68, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.10.012 (verified; PubMed lists it as 2017 e-pub). Automatic serial-sectioning PS-OCT at **3.5 µm in-plane and 50 µm through-plane**. It quantified layer thickness in cerebellar cortex — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.10.012)
- Liu et al. 2022, "Refractive-index matching enhanced polarization sensitive optical coherence tomography quantification in human brain tissue", Biomed Opt Express 13(1):358–372, doi 10.1364/BOE.443066 (verified). TDE index matching cut orientation noise by 56% in WM and 39% in GM, and revealed laminar fibre layers in cortex — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1364/BOE.443066)
- Alkemade et al. 2022, "A unified 3D map of microscopic architecture and MRI of the human brain", Sci Adv 8(17):eabj7892, doi 10.1126/sciadv.abj7892 (verified). Two specimens, each with about 800 histology and IHC sections plus 7T qMRI, all co-aligned **at the 200 µm scale** to the blockface. Openly shared; usable as a validation set for laminar MRI — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abj7892)

### Inferences
- The user's approach fills an apparent gap: no source found here fits depth-field isosurfaces to the laminar bands themselves with ordering and smoothness built in. Published methods either place depth by geometry (Costantini, Nighres) or cluster without spatial priors (Bastiani, Barazany) and then clean up.
- Bastiani's areal-border method, Hotelling's T² between neighbouring profile blocks, is the same idea as the user's "borders are lateral changes in band pattern". It worked at 340 µm on 2–4 layer complexes. That supports treating areal borders as lateral pattern changes rather than depth discontinuities.
- Resolution margin: Fischl lost EC borders between 100 and 120 µm, and Bastiani lost layer 1 at 340 µm. At 160 µm, bands of at least 2–3 voxels (0.3–0.5 mm) are consistent with what Sengupta resolved at 200 µm qT2* (stria plus superficial band). Single-cell-wide layers (EC layer II islands, layer IV of M1) will need priors.
- Validation: OCT or PS-OCT on the block face (Magnain, Wang) gives better registration than Nissl, and the Alkemade data give 200 µm co-registered MRI and histology. The Costantini numbers (306/527 µm) show that MRI–histology registration error can exceed one band thickness. Any µm error claim needs block-face OCT or same-specimen histology.

### Gaps
- No paper reported a laminar surface error in µm against histology. The numbers found are contingency agreement (Bastiani) and registration error (Costantini).
- I found no ex vivo laminar method with joint smoothness, ordering or continuity constraints or per-area template profiles. Wagstyl 2020 (BigBrain, covered elsewhere) is the nearest. If such work exists in MICCAI/IPMI it is not indexed in PubMed, and I did not search there.
- "Massey/Bazin cortical layer detection ex vivo": not found, unverified. The PubMed author search for Massey LA plus cortex MRI returned nothing relevant.
- I did not read the Waehnert 2016 (PMID 26455795), Dinse 2015 (PMID 25896931) or Lifshits 2018 (PMID 28274834, "Resolution considerations in imaging of the cortical layers") full texts. Lifshits is probably the most relevant source on partial volume and resolution, and is worth a follow-up.

## Q2. How methods handle partial volume of thin bands, resolution limits, and layer count changing across areas

### Takeaway
Mostly they don't. Partial volume is handled by coarsening: grouping layers into complexes, using 4 notional depth bins, or segmenting one boundary. Variable layer count is handled implicitly by unsupervised clustering with variable k (Bastiani: k = 3/6, layer IV found only in premotor). No method found models thin layers explicitly as sub-voxel or "riding along" structures.

### Cited Findings
- Bastiani: layer 1 (100–200 µm) was sometimes missing at 340 µm. Layer IV was found in premotor cortex but not in M1, consistent with the debated agranularity of M1. The cluster count was chosen by silhouette and so changed with the data — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5102896/)
- Leuze: 4 notional depth laminae instead of segmented layers — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhs311)
- Costantini: only the infra/supragranular boundary was segmented, despite 150 µm data — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10569704/)
- Fischl: SNR scales with the cube of the linear voxel size, and EC borders were lost from 100 to 120 µm — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2738987/)
- Sengupta: the stria was "somewhat better defined" at 60 than at 100 µm, so finer sampling still helps band sharpness — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5862655/)
- Deistung 2013 found region-dependent laminar patterns in QSM: a high-susceptibility layer (stria) in V1 but a *reduced*-susceptibility layer in temporal cortex. The sign and the count of bands change between areas — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.09.055)

### Inferences
- For the user's model, a per-area template of band count and sign (stria-type paramagnetic and high-R2* band in V1, versus different patterns elsewhere) is supported by Deistung. A single global band model across areas would be wrong.
- Ex vivo R2* depends on cortex orientation to B0 (Q3). Contrast of a band can therefore change laterally with folding, not with area. A lateral pattern-change detector should regress out the orientation angle, or it will place false areal borders on gyral crowns and banks.

### Gaps
- No quantitative partial-volume model of laminar bands in MRI was found. Lifshits 2018 (PMID 28274834) probably covers this; not read.

## Q3. What physically carries laminar contrast in multi-echo GRE ex vivo; is it stronger in R2* than in magnitude; fixation and Fomblin; QSM

### Takeaway
In fixed cortex, R2* (T2*) differences dominate GRE contrast (Fischl 2009). The laminar R2* and frequency contrast is **mainly iron, not myelin**. Fukunaga 2010 showed that iron extraction cut the stria-versus-cortex R2* difference from **13.1 to 2.7 s⁻¹** and the frequency difference from **0.80 to 0.02 Hz**, at 7T, 150 µm, after 6 months in formalin. Iron sits largely in myelin-associated oligodendrocytes and fibres, so it follows myeloarchitecture. Myelin's own diamagnetism *opposes* iron in QSM, which makes susceptibility depth profiles non-monotonic and area-dependent, whereas R1 and R2* fall monotonically from WM to pia (Marques 2017). R2* therefore separates the depth trend from bands best. Magnitude carries the same contrast, scaled by TE, plus S0 (PD and T1) weighting and receive bias.

### Cited Findings
- **Fukunaga et al. 2010**, "Layer-specific variation of iron content in cerebral cortex as a source of MRI contrast", PNAS 107(8):3834–9, doi 10.1073/pnas.0911177107 (verified). Ex vivo slabs: 7T 3D multi-echo GRE, **TR 55 ms, TE 17.5/30.9/44.2 ms, flip 10°, 150 µm isotropic**. 10% formalin for about 6 months, PMI under 24 h, occipital and parietal lobes. Comparison against EM ferritin counts and iron and myelin stains.
  - Ferritin-like particles: line of Gennari 351 ± 27 vs layers II–III 115 ± 51. Iron **50 ± 4 vs 16 ± 7 µg/g wet weight**, a difference of 34 µg/g.
  - Before extraction: R2* inside Gennari **39.6 s⁻¹**, outside **26.5**, WM 46.0. Frequency +0.82, +0.02 and −0.66 Hz.
  - After reductive iron extraction: 17.7, 15.0 and 29.6 s⁻¹. The GM contrast fell from 13.1 to 2.7 s⁻¹ (R2*) and from 0.80 to 0.02 Hz (frequency).
  - Residual contrast, including slight frequency reversal, is attributed to myelin.
  - Iron is "the dominant source of the observed MRI contrast". Iron follows each region's myeloarchitecture. Deeper layers and V2 subcortical WM also show raised R2* and iron — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2840419/)
- **Stüber et al. 2014**, "Myelin and iron concentration in the human brain: a quantitative study of MRI contrast", NeuroImage 93 Pt 1:95–106, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.02.026 (verified). Post mortem block with T1 mapping and T2*w MRI, then PIXE element maps at 1 µm: iron, phosphorus as the myelin proxy, and sulfur for calibration. A **linear model of iron plus myelin** "accurately modeled" the R1 and R2* contrasts. It can be inverted to estimate iron and myelin from MRI ex vivo. The coefficients are in the paper; I did not read them — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.02.026)
- **Kirilina et al. 2020**, "Superficial white matter imaging: Contrast mechanisms and whole-brain in vivo mapping", Sci Adv 6(41):eaaz9281, doi 10.1126/sciadv.aaz9281 (verified). Post mortem temporal lobe, 4% PFA for 6 weeks, then PBS, in a PBS-filled sphere. 7T ME-GRE with **9 echoes, TE1 6.7 ms, ΔTE 8.52 ms, TR 100 ms; 210 µm for R2* and 160 µm for QSM**.
  - Iron (LA-ICP-MS): upper cortex (0–25% depth) **15.6 ± 4 µg/g**, middle cortex (30–65%) **29.2 ± 7**, SWM 55 ± 11, DWM 33 ± 10.
  - In cortical profiles, **iron plus myelin explained 91% of R2* variance and myelin alone 66%**. Iron relaxivity was 0.35 ± 0.25 s⁻¹ per µg/g.
  - De-ironing reduced SWM R2* by 12 ± 2 s⁻¹ (about 32% of total) and R2 by 6 s⁻¹ (about 22%). Iron therefore weighs more on R2* than on R2, through R2′ mesoscale dephasing.
  - Layer IV showed raised R2*, R2 and susceptibility in post mortem profiles (shown in a figure, not quantified in the text).
  - Iron hotspots colocalize with oligodendrocytes; modelling says iron in fibres dominates the R2* effect.
  - Orientation terms explained 8 ± 3% of whole-brain R2* variance in vivo. Post mortem, some R2* depended on orientation — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541072/)
- **Hametner et al. 2018**, NeuroImage 179:117–133, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.06.007 (verified). Unfixed in situ brains at 7T, then ferrozine on 671 punches and stains in 3331 ROIs. QSM vs ferrozine iron r = 0.755 and R2* r = 0.738. R2* vs iron stain intensity r = 0.619, QSM 0.445. **Myelin correlates negatively with QSM (r = −0.352), a diamagnetic effect.** Mostly deep GM, not cortical layers — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2018.06.007)
- **Deistung et al. 2013**, "Toward in vivo histology: a comparison of quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) with magnitude-, phase-, and R2*-imaging at ultra-high magnetic field strength", NeuroImage 65:299–314, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.09.055 (verified). In vivo 7T. QSM showed substructures "not generally visible on magnitude, frequency or R2*-maps". In V1 there is a high-susceptibility layer (stria); temporal cortex has a *reduced*-susceptibility layer — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.09.055)
- **Marques, Khabipova & Gruetter 2017**, "Studying cyto and myeloarchitecture of the human cortex at ultra-high field with quantitative imaging: R1, R2* and magnetic susceptibility", NeuroImage 147:152–163, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.12.009 (verified). In vivo, 0.65 mm. **R1 and R2* decrease monotonically from WM to pia; susceptibility has "a more complex behaviour"** because iron and myelin act in opposite directions — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.12.009)
- **Duyn et al. 2007**, "High-field MRI of brain cortical substructure based on signal phase", PNAS 104(28):11796–801, doi 10.1073/pnas.0610821104 (verified). 7T GRE phase at 0.24 × 0.24 × 1.0 mm. GM/WM phase CNR was 3:1 to 20:1, about 10× magnitude. GM phase contrast was "suggestive of the underlying layer structure" and is attributed partly to iron — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0610821104)
- **Cohen-Adad et al. 2012**, "T2* mapping and B0 orientation-dependence at 7 T reveal cyto- and myeloarchitecture organization of the human cortex", NeuroImage 60(2):1006–14, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.01.053 (verified). In vivo, multi-echo. Cortical T2* was **32.20 ± 1.35 ms**. **Orientation dependence was strongest in M1 (ΔR2* = 4.10 s⁻¹)** — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.01.053)
- **Gulban et al. 2022**, "Mesoscopic in vivo human T2* dataset acquired using quantitative MRI at 7 Tesla", NeuroImage 264:119733, doi 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119733 (verified). In vivo **0.35 mm isotropic** T2*, with depth-dependent T2*/R2* in V1 and auditory cortex consistent across subjects. Open data at OSF N5BJ7 — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119733)
- **Fixation**: Birkl et al. 2016, "Effects of formalin fixation and temperature on MR relaxation times in the human brain", NMR Biomed 29(4):458–65, doi 10.1002/nbm.3477 (verified). After 190 days in formalin, **all relaxation times decreased, T1 by up to 76%**. Water loss was only 1–6%, so cross-linking is the cause. The temperature coefficients also change (T1's falls, T2's rises) — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.3477). Fischl 2009 fixed-tissue values: T2* GM 26 ms, WM 11 ms; T1 GM 1078 ms, WM 770 ms — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2738987/). Sengupta (fixed, then PBS-washed for 6 weeks): GM T2* is higher than in vivo, possibly from rehydration and no venous blood — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5862655/)
- Fatterpekar 2002: hypointensity peaks at maximum myelin *and* maximum cell density. Cell-dense layers (e.g. IV, II) can therefore also be dark — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12223371/)

### Inferences
These are my calculations from the cited numbers applied to the user's protocol and are not themselves sourced.
- **Echo choice.** Use Fukunaga's ex vivo stria R2* of 39.6 s⁻¹ against 26.5 s⁻¹ for the surrounding cortex (ΔR2* = 13.1 s⁻¹). The relative magnitude contrast is then 1 − exp(−ΔR2*·TE): about **12% at 9.37 ms, 24% at 20.71 ms and 34% at 32.05 ms**. The CNR of the difference S0·[exp(−R2a·TE) − exp(−R2b·TE)] peaks near TE ≈ 1/mean(R2*) ≈ 30 ms. The user's third echo is near optimal and the first carries mainly S0. This agrees with Fischl's CNR-optimal TE of 20 ms for EC (GM T2* 26 ms) and with Sengupta's TE about 20 ms for 100/60 µm anatomy.
- **R2* vs magnitude.** R2* from a 3-echo fit removes S0, which carries PD, T1 weighting (near Ernst: for GM T1 of about 1078 ms at TR 80 ms the Ernst angle is about 22°, against the user's 25°) and the receive (B1−) bias. It also turns the TE-dependent magnitude contrast into a single quantity, so the band contrast is not divided across echoes. With only 3 echoes, R2* noise will be larger than the noise of the best single magnitude echo. A feature space of {S0, R2*}, or the three log-magnitudes, which is linear in R2* for monoexponential decay, keeps all of the information. Fatterpekar's cell-density darkening will appear partly in S0 (PD/T1) and partly in R2*. Keeping S0 as a separate channel may help to separate cell-dense from iron/myelin-rich bands.
- **QSM.** It separates paramagnetic iron from diamagnetic myelin and can change sign between areas (Deistung, Hametner, Marques). That makes it informative for areal templates. It is harder as a depth feature, because the profile is non-monotonic and background-field removal is a problem at a sample edge. The user acquires magnitude only, so QSM is unavailable unless phase is saved. Duyn's 10× phase CNR suggests that keeping phase is worth it for future scans.
- **Orientation.** R2* depends on fibre orientation to B0 (Cohen-Adad 4.10 s⁻¹ in M1 in vivo; Kirilina post mortem). Laminar band contrast in R2* will therefore vary with local cortical orientation. In a lateral pattern-change model this is a nuisance that can create false areal borders. Include θ, the angle between the cortical normal and B0, as a covariate in the per-area template.
- **Iron and myelin.** For the user's "laminar contrast bands", R2* bands are mostly an iron map that follows myelin (Fukunaga, Kirilina 91% vs 66%). Myeloarchitectonic band structure is therefore the right mental model, but areas with iron–myelin decoupling (age, pathology) may show shifted bands.

### Gaps
- **Fomblin**: I found no source in this session that quantifies Fomblin's effect on cortical R2* or laminar contrast. Sengupta used Fluorinert FC-3283, Kirilina used PBS, and Fischl did not state the medium. Physics expectation (unsourced): Fomblin is proton-free and roughly susceptibility-matched, so the main risks are air bubbles and edge susceptibility, which affect superficial R2* near the pia.
- Ex vivo R2* of the stria in other fixation regimes (PFA vs formalin, duration) is not quantified beyond Fukunaga (formalin, 6 months) and Kirilina (PFA, 6 weeks; no stria numbers).
- The Stüber 2014 regression coefficients for iron and myelin in R1 and R2* were not extracted (full text not read).
- Laminar R2* values outside V1, such as the Baillarger bands in non-primary cortex, were not found as numbers. Kirilina only shows layer IV elevation in a figure.

## Q4. Methods that fit layer surfaces jointly with smoothness / continuity constraints, or use a per-area template profile

### Takeaway
No ex vivo or in vivo MRI method found in this round fits laminar surfaces jointly with smoothness or continuity constraints to band contrast. Topology-preserving multi-object level sets (Bazin 2014 MGDM) build topology and ordering into the representation, but only for tissue classes. Per-area profile templates appear only implicitly. Bastiani compares profile blocks for areal borders, and Dinse 2015 (not read) builds a cytoarchitecture-driven myelin model per area. BigBrain (Wagstyl 2020, covered elsewhere) is the closest precedent, with surfaces fitted to histology-derived profiles.

### Cited Findings
- Bazin et al. 2014: multi-object topology-preserving deformable models with shape and intensity atlases. Topology is a hard constraint in the representation, not a post-processing step — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.03.077)
- Bastiani et al. 2016: areal borders from profile-signature change (Mahalanobis or Hotelling's T²). The layer clusters have no spatial prior — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5102896/)
- Dinse et al. 2015, NeuroImage 114 (PMID 25896931; title from memory "A cytoarchitecture-driven myelin model reveals area-specific signatures in human primary and secondary areas using ultra-high resolution in-vivo brain MRI"; **title unverified**, not read). This is likely the best "per-area template profile" precedent in MRI.
- Waehnert et al. 2016, NeuroImage 125 (PMID 26455795, title unverified: "A subject-specific framework for in vivo myeloarchitectonic analysis using high resolution quantitative MRI"), not read.

### Inferences
- The user's design has the depth field T, ordering implied by T's monotonicity, lateral smoothness of T, and band-pattern templates that may change laterally only. It puts into the energy the constraints that others add as fix-ups. From the tables in Q1:
  - Ordering removes Bastiani's need to map clusters to layers by hand.
  - A single T field removes cluster noise at the boundaries if pial and WM partial-volume classes are modelled.
  - Lateral template smoothness with sparse breaks removes threshold-based areal border detection.
  - Ride-along thin layers remove the missing-layer-1 failure.

### Gaps
- No MICCAI/IPMI search was done. Joint laminar surface fitting may exist in the computer-vision literature (e.g. layered-surface graph cuts as in retinal OCT layer segmentation, which are a strong analogue with ordering and smoothness constraints built in). Retinal OCT multi-surface graph search (Garvin/Li et al.) is a likely precedent and was not verified in this session.
