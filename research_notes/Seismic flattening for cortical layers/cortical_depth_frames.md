# Cortical depth coordinate systems and contrast-driven laminar surfaces

Scope: the neuroimaging/neuroanatomy side of replacing a geometric depth frame (fast-marching distance D from the white surface, streamlines along grad D, DP-fitted band depths per region) with one fitted scalar field T whose isosurfaces follow laminar contrast (seismic-flattening analogue). Research budget was about 15 tool calls. Items marked **[unverified]** come from prior knowledge and were not confirmed against a primary record in this session.

## (a) Geometric depth frames: assumptions, validation, residual errors

### Takeaway
All standard frames (equidistant, Laplace, equivolume) are purely geometric: they use only the two boundary surfaces (and their curvature). Equivolume (Bok 1929, made operational by Waehnert et al. 2014) fits myelinated bands in MRI better than Laplace or equidistant, mainly at gyral crowns and sulcal fundi. But histology-scale work on BigBrain shows that a single equivolumetric surface lies in different layers in different areas, because relative layer depth also depends on areal cytoarchitecture. So no geometric frame can, by construction, follow laminar boundaries across areal borders.

### Cited Findings
- **Waehnert et al. 2014.** Waehnert MD, Dinse J, Weiss M, Streicher MN, Waehnert P, Geyer S, Turner R, Bazin P-L. "Anatomically motivated modeling of cortical laminae." *NeuroImage* 93(Pt 2):210–220, 2014. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.03.078. Authors and pages confirmed from the PubMed/Duke records. The full author list is **[unverified]**; the search snippets gave only the first-author-led record. — [PubMed 23603284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23603284/); [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1818914)
  - Claim: the equipotentials that Laplacian profiles are built from, and equidistant laminae, "do not follow the anatomical layers observed" in high-resolution MRI of cadaver brain. — [PubMed 23603284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23603284/)
  - Model: equivolume, from Bok (1929). Cortical segments keep their volume while layer thickness changes with folding. Depth isocontours were compared with myelinated bands (stria of Gennari / Baillarger bands) in ex vivo and in vivo MRI. — [PubMed 23603284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23603284/)
  - Result (qualitative in the abstract): compared with Laplace equipotentials and a "simplified model that preserves lamina thicknesses" (equidistant), equivolume gives "a significant improvement both in precision and accuracy". I could not retrieve the full text, so I have no μm error numbers. — [Kleinnijenhuis OHBM 2014 slides citing Waehnert](https://users.fmrib.ox.ac.uk/~michielk/MendeleyLibrary/presentations/Kleinnijenhuis_OHBM-2014_presentation.pdf); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23603284/)
  - Direction of the Laplace error (from a secondary summary of the Waehnert/ISMRM 2012 work): the stria lies closer to white matter than the Laplace contours at sulcal fundi, and closer to the pial surface at gyral crowns. Laplace cannot fit both at once. — [ISMRM 2012 abstract 1085](https://archive.ismrm.org/2012/1085.html) (search-snippet level; abstract not opened)
- **Nighres implementation** of Waehnert: `nighres.laminar.volumetric_layering(inner_levelset, outer_levelset, n_layers, method='volume-preserving'|'distance-preserving', layer_dir, curv_scale=3)`. Inputs are level sets of the GM/WM and GM/CSF surfaces. Outputs are continuous depth 0→1, discrete layers and boundary level sets. Curvature is estimated over a window of `curv_scale` voxels. The original Java module is by Waehnert, Bazin and Dinse. — [Nighres docs](https://nighres.readthedocs.io/en/latest/laminar/volumetric_layering.html)
- **Leprince et al. 2015 (highres-cortex).** Leprince Y, Poupon F, Delzescaux T, Hasboun D, Poupon C, Rivière D. "Combined Laplacian-equivolumic model for studying cortical lamination with ultra high field MRI (7 T)." *IEEE ISBI 2015*, pp. 580–583. Layers follow the equivolumic principle, and the "vertical" (columnar) structure follows Laplace streamlines. Profiles keep the original voxel sampling (computed in voxel space, not on meshes). Validated on a post-mortem human brain at 7 T, 0.35 mm. The co-author list is **[unverified]** beyond the first author and the HAL record. — [HAL cea-01119475](https://hal-cea.archives-ouvertes.fr/cea-01119475); software: [neurospin/highres-cortex](https://github.com/neurospin/highres-cortex), [BrainVISA "Compute equivolumetric depth"](https://brainvisa.info/axon/en/processes/isovolume.html). Input is a voxel classification image, with an advection step size tuned to voxel size. Note that this is architecturally very close to the user's current D + streamlines frame.
- **Jones, Buchbinder & Aharon 2000.** "Three-dimensional mapping of cortical thickness using Laplace's equation." *Human Brain Mapping* 11(1):12–32. The Laplace solution between the two surfaces gives non-crossing streamlines, and thickness is measured along them. **[unverified in this session; citation from prior knowledge]**. Assumption: columns are the gradient lines of a harmonic function. There is no anatomical basis for equipotentials as layers, which is what Waehnert later criticized.
- **Bok 1929.** Bok ST, "Der Einfluß der in den Furchen und Windungen auftretenden Krümmungen der Großhirnrinde auf die Rindenarchitektur," *Z. Gesamte Neurol. Psychiatr.* 121:682–750. **[unverified; title/venue from prior knowledge]**. The principle is that the volume fraction of each layer within a cortical column segment is preserved under folding. On the convex side (crown) the deep layers thicken and the superficial layers thin, and the reverse holds in fundi. — principle as restated in [PubMed 23603284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23603284/)
- **Equidistant vs equivolume divergence is greatest where curvature is high.** The two coincide in flat cortex and differ most at gyral crowns and sulcal fundi. BrainVoyager recommends equivolume at ≤0.5 mm isotropic because equidistant sampling misassigns layers in highly curved cortex. — [BrainVoyager equivolume docs](https://download.brainvoyager.com/bv/doc/UsersGuide/HighResDataAnalysis/EquiVolumeDepthModelling.html)
- **LayNii (Huber et al. 2021).** Huber LR, Poser BA, Bandettini PA, et al. "LayNii: A software suite for layer-fMRI." *NeuroImage* 237:118091, 2021. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.118091. A C/C++ command-line suite. `LN2_LAYERS` generates "equi-distant or equi-volume layers from gray matter segmentation" in native voxel space. — [Maastricht CRIS record](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/laynii-a-software-suite-for-layer-fmri/); [LayNii GitHub](https://github.com/layerfMRI/LAYNII). The algorithm itself is on a linked page I did not read. My recollection is that LN2_LAYERS computes geodesic distances from the inner and outer rim and then derives an equivolume factor from local "metric" thickness, with smoothing **[unverified]**. There is no accuracy validation against histology in the README.
- **Younes, Kutten & Ratnanather 2019.** "Normal and Equivolumetric Coordinate Systems for Cortical Areas." arXiv:1911.07999 (math.OC; preprint, no journal listed). They estimate coordinates between two surfaces by diffeomorphic registration under geometric priors ("streamline normality or equivolumetric conditions on layers"). Simulations are compared with other methods. The abstract has no quantitative results. — [arXiv 1911.07999](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07999). Relevance: this is a variational formulation of the depth frame. A data (contrast) term could in principle be added to it, but they do not do this.
- **Residual error vs true layers (histology).** Wagstyl et al. 2018 found that on BigBrain, "there was almost no change in equivolumetric depth with cortical curvature" (β = −1.07). So equivolume removes the curvature effect, as Bok predicts. But layer IV relative depth "is determined both by local morphology and by inter-regional differences in cytoarchitecture". Layer IV is relatively deeper in V1 and parts of medial prefrontal cortex, and "a single equivolumetric surface is located in different cortical layers of different cortical areas." — [Wagstyl et al. 2018, PMC5998962](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5998962/)

### Inferences
- The user's constant-speed D from the white surface is an equidistant-from-one-side frame. It is worse than symmetric equidistant at crowns and fundi, because it ignores the pial surface's curvature. Expected bias is of the Waehnert type: in fundi the true bands sit deeper than predicted and at crowns more superficial, in relative-depth terms. The user's per-region DP band depths partly absorb areal differences but not within-region curvature variation.
- Even a perfect equivolume frame leaves an areal component in band depth (Wagstyl 2018). This is the strongest argument for a contrast-fitted T: geometry corrects curvature, but only data can correct areal differences.
- A practical hybrid suggested by the literature: initialize T as equivolume depth (Waehnert/Nighres or highres-cortex), then let it deform toward the contrast with a smoothness prior. Wagstyl 2018 did a surface-wise version of exactly this (see (b)).

### Gaps
- No μm-level accuracy numbers for Waehnert 2014 (full text not retrieved). I found no study that gives histology-validated boundary error (μm) for equivolume vs equidistant vs Laplace separately in sulci vs gyri. The Wagstyl 2018 regression is the closest.
- LN2_LAYERS algorithm details are not verified.
- Huntenburg et al. "Nighres" paper (GigaScience 2018) was not looked up **[unverified]**.

## (b) Methods that adapt depth coordinates or layer surfaces to observed laminar contrast

### Takeaway
Several methods place individual layer surfaces from profile contrast: a rule-based peak/inflection search (Wagstyl 2018), a 1D CNN per profile (Wagstyl 2020), and 3D U-Nets on ex vivo MRI (Zeng 2024). None that I found fits a continuous depth coordinate (a scalar field whose level sets are all layer boundaries) to the contrast. Profiles are always sampled along fixed geometric streamlines or vertex pairs. Layers are then found as labels or transitions along each profile, and lateral regularity comes from post-hoc mesh smoothing. I found no published dynamic-time-warping or "profile registration" method for cortical laminar profiles. A fitted-T approach therefore appears novel in this field (absence of evidence from a limited search).

### Cited Findings
- **Wagstyl et al. 2018.** Wagstyl K, Lepage C, Bludau S, Zilles K, Fletcher PC, Amunts K, Evans AC. "Mapping Cortical Laminar Structure in the 3D BigBrain." *Cerebral Cortex* 28(7):2551–2562, 2018. doi:10.1093/cercor/bhy074. The authors beyond Wagstyl are **[unverified]**; title, venue, pages and DOI were confirmed. — [PMC5998962](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5998962/)
  - Profiles: 100 equidistant points between linked pial/white vertices, on a 40 μm anisotropically smoothed volume. A neural net was used only for tissue classification, with over 100 samples per class.
  - Layer I/II boundary: moved to the nearest inflection point of each profile, then the surface was smoothed.
  - Layer IV: initialized at the first major peak after the II/III upward inflection. It was then refined iteratively: mesh smoothing, smoothing of equivolumetric depth (10 mm FWHM), and moving each vertex to the nearest intensity peak. This stopped when fewer than 100 of 163,842 vertices moved. **This is the closest thing found to "fit a depth surface to contrast with a smoothness prior": an alternating data-snap and smoothing scheme in equivolumetric-depth space.**
  - Accuracy vs manual (37 regions, 7 sections): layer I/II signed error −13 μm (SD 40, unsigned 63 μm). Layer IV signed error −11 μm (SD 68, unsigned 72 μm). Layer IV relative depth vs manual has mean Pearson r = 0.72.
  - Layer IV relative depth vs mean curvature: a cubic fit is best by BIC. Layer IV is more superficial at crowns and deeper in fundi, consistent with Bok.
- **Wagstyl et al. 2020.** Wagstyl K, Larocque S, Cucurull G, Lepage C, Cohen JP, Bludau S, Palomero-Gallagher N, Lewis LB, Funck T, Spitzer H, Dickscheid T, Fletcher PC, Romero A, Zilles K, Amunts K, Bengio Y, Evans AC. "BigBrain 3D atlas of cortical layers: Cortical and laminar thickness gradients diverge in sensory and motor cortices." *PLoS Biology* 18(4):e3000678, 2020. doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3000678. Full author list **[unverified]**; DOI, title and venue confirmed. — [PMC7159250](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7159250/)
  - Training: manual six-layer segmentations of 51 regions in 13 BigBrain sections (rescanned at 5 μm), giving more than 500,000 training profiles.
  - Profiles: 200 **equidistant (linear)** points between pial and white vertex pairs. Pial-to-white vectors were smoothed (3 mm FWHM) to reduce profile crossings, and surfaces were extended about 0.5 mm. No equivolume / Bok model was used in sampling.
  - Network: a 1D CNN (6 blocks, filter size 49) classifies each profile point into 8 classes (background, I–VI, WM). Loss is median-frequency-weighted cross-entropy. **Each profile is classified independently**, with no lateral coupling in the network.
  - Surfaces: class transitions give the boundary vertices. Failures (<1%) were interpolated, then 0.5 mm FWHM smoothing and 20 iterations of shrinkage-free mesh smoothing were applied.
  - Accuracy: per-point accuracy on held-out folds was 83% ± 2% (before postprocessing). It falls with resolution: 85% at 20 μm, **76% at 100 μm**, 60% at 1000 μm. This is directly relevant to the user's 160 μm. Layer IV had the lowest confidence.
- **Leprince 2015 / highres-cortex** and **Nighres** compute depth from geometry only. No data term was found in their documentation. — [highres-cortex](https://github.com/neurospin/highres-cortex); [Nighres](https://nighres.readthedocs.io/en/latest/laminar/volumetric_layering.html)
- **Fracasso et al. 2016.** Fracasso A, van Veluw SJ, Visser F, Luijten PR, Spliet W, Zwanenburg JJM, Dumoulin SO, Petridou N. "Lines of Baillarger in vivo and ex vivo: Myelin contrast across lamina at 7T MRI and histology." *NeuroImage* 133:163–175, 2016. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.02.072. Co-authors **[unverified]**. A modified T1w MPRAGE enhances intracortical myelin contrast. It was validated ex vivo against histology. In vivo, the calcarine profile matches the stria of Gennari, and similar profiles in extra-calcarine (parietal, frontal) cortex are interpreted as Baillarger lines. The data are in *Data in Brief* 8:990–1003 (doi:10.1016/j.dib.2016.06.058). This is detection on depth profiles; it is not a depth-coordinate fit. — [UMC Utrecht record](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/lines-of-baillarger-in-vivo-and-ex-vivo-myelin-contrast-across-la/); [Data in Brief PMC4961349](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961349)
- **Zeng et al. 2024**, *Cerebral Cortex*. A semi-supervised multi-resolution U-Net labels supra- vs infragranular layers in whole-hemisphere ex vivo 7T MRI (17 hemispheres, 120 μm), with Dice > 0.8. Author list and DOI **[unverified]**; found via the UCL Discovery accepted manuscript. This is a voxel segmentation, not a depth fit. — [UCL Discovery 10199686](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10199686)
- **Weiss et al. (ISMRM 2011)**: a semi-automated in vivo 7T V1 segmentation using laminar profiles containing the stria of Gennari. — [ISMRM 2011 #2359](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/11MProceedings/PDFfiles/2359.pdf) (search-snippet level)
- **Dinse et al. 2015** (also in (c)) fits a *model* of the myelin profile shape per area from cytoarchitecture to quantitative T1 profiles in an equivolume frame. The depth frame itself is not adapted. — [Duke Scholars record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1818924)
- Search for DTW or registration of laminar profiles found only volume/surface registration (e.g. Postelnicu, Zöllei & Fischl, IEEE TMI 2009, combined surface+volume registration), not depth-profile warping. — [PMC2761957](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2761957)

### Inferences
- The closest precedent for a fitted T is Wagstyl 2018's alternating "snap layer IV vertex to the nearest peak" and "smooth equivolumetric depth" loop. It is a block-coordinate method for one isosurface. A fitted T generalizes it to all isosurfaces at once with monotonicity in depth, which is the seismic-flattening setup.
- Wagstyl 2020's resolution curve (76% per-point accuracy at 100 μm, vs 85% at 20 μm) suggests that at 160 μm MRI, per-column layer calls will be noisy. That argues for strong lateral coupling (a smooth T), not per-column decisions. The user's per-region DP is a coarse version of that coupling.
- Equidistant sampling along smoothed pial–white vectors (Wagstyl 2020) plus a learned classifier sidesteps the frame choice: the network absorbs curvature effects. A contrast-fitted T would do the same explicitly.

## (c) Areal border detection from laminar profiles: edges or clusters?

### Takeaway
The dominant histology method (Schleicher, Amunts, Zilles; Jülich) finds borders as **edges**: local maxima of a Mahalanobis distance between adjacent blocks of profile-shape features, swept along the cortex, then tested for significance and checked for consistency across sections. Deep-learning successors (Spitzer, Schiffer) do **region classification** (labels per pixel or patch). MRI work includes both edge (abrupt profile change) and clustering formulations. The user's Ward merging is a region/cluster formulation; Schleicher-style edge detection is the standard alternative and could validate it.

### Cited Findings
- **Schleicher et al. observer-independent method.** Origin: Schleicher A, Amunts K, Geyer S, Kowalski T, Zilles K, "An observer-independent cytoarchitectonic mapping of the human cortex using a stereological approach," *Acta Stereologica* 1998 (applied to occipital cortex). It confirmed visually defined borders and "revealed new ones which had not been detected visually". The Mahalanobis distance was more sensitive than Euclidean. — [Acta Stereologica](https://popups.uliege.be/0351-580x/index.php?id=2499). Full method paper: Schleicher A, Amunts K, Geyer S, Morosan P, Zilles K, "Observer-independent method for microstructural parcellation of cerebral cortex: a quantitative approach to cytoarchitectonics," *NeuroImage* 9:165–177, 1999 **[unverified in this session]**. Schleicher et al. 2005 *Anat Embryol* "Quantitative architectural analysis: a new approach to cortical mapping" **[unverified]**.
  - Pipeline: compute the gray level index (GLI, the cell-body volume fraction). Extract GLI profiles along traverses perpendicular to the cortex. Compute ~10 central-moment shape features per profile. Compute the Mahalanobis distance between adjacent blocks of profiles, sliding along the cortex. **Borders are significant local maxima** of the distance function. — [PMC figure legend PMC3429851](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3429851/figure/F3); [BigBrain OHBM 2012 poster](https://bigbrain.loris.ca/papers/Poster-A0-OHBM-2012.pdf)
  - Block sizes of 10–24 profiles are analysed for reliability. — [OHBM 2012 poster](https://bigbrain.loris.ca/papers/Poster-A0-OHBM-2012.pdf)
  - Significance: Hotelling's T² with Bonferroni correction. Borders are accepted only if they are consistent on consecutive sections and verified on histology. — summarized from search results on Jülich parahippocampal mapping (secondary)
  - Alternative statistics: Schmitt, Hömke & Dümbgen 2003, excess-mass differences for transition regions. — [Schmitt 2003 PDF](https://neuroviisas.med.uni-rostock.de/publications/Schmitt-2003.pdf)
- **Dinse et al. 2015.** Dinse J, Härtwich N, Waehnert MD, Tardif CL, Schäfer A, Geyer S, Preim B, Turner R, Bazin P-L. "A cytoarchitecture-driven myelin model reveals area-specific signatures in human primary and secondary areas using ultra-high resolution in-vivo brain MRI." *NeuroImage* 114:71–87, 2015. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2015.04.023. Co-author list **[unverified]**. It predicts laminar myelin (T1) profiles from known cytoarchitecture, compares them quantitatively with 7T in vivo data (9 subjects), and validates on one post-mortem sample with ex vivo MRI and histology. Profiles are area-specific for primary and secondary areas. The authors raise the question of whether cyto- and myeloarchitectonic boundaries coincide. Precursor: MICCAI 2013, LNCS 8150:51–58. — [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1818924); [MICCAI 2013](https://iacl.ece.jhu.edu/proceedings/miccai2013/html/8150/81500051.htm)
- **Schiffer et al. 2021.** Schiffer C, Spitzer H, Kiwitz K, Unger N, Wagstyl K, Evans AC, Harmeling S, Amunts K, Dickscheid T. "Convolutional neural networks for cytoarchitectonic brain mapping at large scale." *NeuroImage* 240:118327, 2021. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.118327; arXiv:2011.12857. Co-author list **[unverified]**. A CNN is trained on one pair of annotated sections and labels the sections between them. It is faster than the observer-independent workflow, needs no 3D reconstruction, and is robust to artefacts. This is a **region (segmentation) formulation**. — [arXiv 2011.12857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12857)
- Spitzer et al. 2018 MICCAI, "Improving cytoarchitectonic segmentation of human brain areas with self-supervised Siamese networks" **[unverified]**. Self-supervised pretraining on predicting 3D distance between patches, then area classification.
- **Bastiani et al. 2016**, *Frontiers in Neuroscience* 10:487, doi:10.3389/fnins.2016.00487. "Automatic segmentation of human cortical layer-complexes and architectural areas using ex vivo diffusion MRI…" (exact title **[unverified]**). Unsupervised clustering of high-res ex vivo dMRI in human M1/premotor cortex. The depth-wise dMRI signature locates area borders where it changes abruptly. Checked against histology. This combines **clustering and edge** ideas. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2016.00487/full)

### Inferences
- The edge formulation (Schleicher) needs a 1D (or 2D) traversal order and a block size. The cluster formulation (Ward, CNN labels) needs no explicit ordering but can fragment. In MRI at 160 μm, a Mahalanobis edge map on band features over the column graph could serve as an independent check on Ward-merged region borders.
- Profile features in Schleicher are moments of the profile **in a fixed depth frame**. Frame errors (curvature) therefore create spurious profile-shape changes, which is why equivolume or contrast-fitted depth matters for border detection too. A fitted T that flattens layers would reduce curvature-driven false borders, but it could also absorb true areal changes in band depth if it is over-regularized. Keep areal depth shifts identifiable, for example by letting T bend at borders or by analysing T's metric (band thicknesses) as a feature.

- Amunts/Zilles reviews on the observer-independent method (e.g. Amunts & Zilles 2015 *Neuron* "Architectonic mapping of the human brain beyond Brodmann") **[unverified]**.
- No quantitative comparison of edge vs cluster areal-border accuracy was found.

## Equivolume vs equidistant vs Laplace: accuracy numbers

### Takeaway
Hard numbers are scarce. Waehnert 2014 reports "significant" improvement in precision and accuracy for equivolume, but I could not retrieve its effect sizes. The best histology numbers available are from BigBrain: layer surfaces fitted to the data have about 63–72 μm unsigned error vs manual, equivolumetric depth is nearly curvature-invariant, but areal differences remain.

### Cited Findings
- Waehnert 2014: equivolume beats Laplace and equidistant "both in precision and accuracy" (qualitative). — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23603284/)
- Wagstyl 2018: data-driven layer IV surface has unsigned error 72 μm. Equivolumetric depth changes very little with curvature (β = −1.07). A single equivolume surface crosses layers between areas. — [PMC5998962](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5998962/)
- Wagstyl 2020: per-point layer classification accuracy is 83% ± 2% (20 μm) and 76% at 100 μm. — [PMC7159250](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7159250/)
- Methods diverge most at crowns and fundi, and equidistant misassigns layers at ≤0.5 mm in high-curvature cortex. — [BrainVoyager](https://download.brainvoyager.com/bv/doc/UsersGuide/HighResDataAnalysis/EquiVolumeDepthModelling.html)

### Inferences
- At 160 μm voxels, a 70 μm boundary error is about half a voxel. Equivolume-vs-equidistant differences at crowns and fundi are probably of order 10–30% of thickness (a typical figure, **not sourced here**), i.e. several voxels in 2.5–4 mm cortex. So the frame choice matters at this resolution.

### Gaps
- No table of μm errors for the three frames against histology was found. The Waehnert full text, and possibly Kleinnijenhuis et al. 2015 (BA17 equivolume validation, mentioned in a search snippet), are where to look next.
