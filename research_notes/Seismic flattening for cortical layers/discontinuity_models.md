# Discontinuity-preserving models (line processes, weak membrane, Mumford–Shah, Potts) and edge- vs region-based cortical parcellation

Verification legend: **[V]** = title/authors/venue (and DOI where given) confirmed by a web source this session. **[V-partial]** = paper confirmed, some detail (e.g., DOI) not confirmed. **[U]** = from background knowledge, not web-verified this session; check before citing.

Notation used throughout, mapped to the baillarger setting: graph G = (V, E), V = cortical columns over the white surface, E = neighbour pairs (i, j) with weights w_ij. Data f_i ∈ R^d per column = laminar band boundary depths (d = number of band boundaries). Fitted field u_i ∈ R^d. A "spring" on edge (i, j) is a penalty ρ(u_i − u_j).

## Key Question 1: Classical formulations and solvers (Geman & Geman, Blake & Zisserman / GNC, Mumford–Shah, Ambrosio–Tortorelli, Black & Rangarajan, Potts / graph cuts, convex relaxations), and which give closed boundaries by construction

### Takeaway
All these models are one family: a data term plus a pairwise penalty that is quadratic for small differences and saturates (truncated quadratic) or is constant for any nonzero difference (Potts / L0). An explicit "line process" l_ij ∈ {0,1} (or [0,1]) on each edge is mathematically the same as a truncated/robust penalty with l eliminated (Blake–Zisserman; Black–Rangarajan). Closed boundaries are guaranteed only by **label-based** (partition) formulations, Potts / piecewise-constant Mumford–Shah, multilabel graph cuts, convex minimal-partition relaxations, cut pursuit. In these the boundary is defined as {edges whose endpoints have different labels}. **Edge-based** variables (line processes, weak membrane, piecewise-smooth Mumford–Shah, Ambrosio–Tortorelli) can produce open "cracks" that end inside a region.

### Cited Findings

**Geman & Geman 1984 (line process, MRF/Gibbs, annealing)** [V]
- S. Geman, D. Geman, "Stochastic Relaxation, Gibbs Distributions, and the Bayesian Restoration of Images," IEEE TPAMI 6(6):721–741, 1984, DOI 10.1109/TPAMI.1984.4767596 — [Wikipedia: Stuart Geman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Geman); [PDF](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~mannr/cs886-w10/GemanandGeman84.pdf)
- Pixel intensities and edge presence/orientation are treated as states of a lattice system. An energy defines a Gibbs distribution (equivalently, an MRF), and the MAP estimate is computed by stochastic relaxation with simulated annealing. "Line Process" is one of the indexed descriptors. — [PDF](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~mannr/cs886-w10/GemanandGeman84.pdf); [library record](https://library.iut.ac.ir/Inventory/104/2768.htm)
- Formulation (standard form, [U] for exact notation): E(u, l) = Σ_i (u_i − f_i)² + λ Σ_(ij) (1 − l_ij)(u_i − u_j)² + α Σ_(ij) l_ij + V_line(l). The extra clique potential V_line on configurations of neighbouring line variables penalizes dangling line ends and favours continuation. This is the original 1984 remedy for **unclosed boundaries**: closure comes from a soft prior on line configurations, not from construction.

**Blake & Zisserman 1987, "Visual Reconstruction" (weak string / weak membrane, GNC)** [U, not web-verified this session; MIT Press, 1987]
- Weak membrane: E(u, l) = Σ (u_i − f_i)² + λ² Σ_(ij) (u_i − u_j)²(1 − l_ij) + α Σ l_ij. Minimizing over l in closed form gives the **truncated quadratic** g(t) = min(λ² t², α). This is the user's "spring that breaks": the spring breaks when |u_i − u_j| > √α / λ.
- Graduated non-convexity (GNC): replace g with a family g^(p), where p = 1 gives a convex approximation (the quadratic segments are joined by a concave spline with a curvature bound tied to the data-term stiffness) and p → 0 gives g. Minimize while p decreases, warm-starting each stage. This is a deterministic continuation alternative to annealing. It is not guaranteed to reach the global optimum in general but behaves well in practice. A weak "string" (1D) breaks in a deterministic, order-independent way only if the whole energy is minimized jointly.
- The membrane (first-order) penalizes gradient; the weak **plate** (second-order) also allows creases (gradient discontinuities). This is relevant if laminar depths may change slope without jumping.

**Mumford & Shah 1989** [V]
- D. Mumford, J. Shah, "Optimal approximations by piecewise smooth functions and associated variational problems," Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 42(5):577–685, 1989, DOI 10.1002/cpa.3160420503 — [Harvard DASH PDF](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/handle/1/3637121/mumford_optimalapproxpiece.pdf?sequence=1); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumford%E2%80%93Shah_functional)
- Functional (standard): E(u, K) = ∫_Ω (u − f)² + μ ∫_(Ω∖K) |∇u|² + ν H¹(K). K is the discontinuity set and H¹ its length. It is the continuum limit of the weak membrane. Piecewise-constant limit (μ → ∞): minimal partition / Potts. [formula: standard; U for notation]
- Pathology [U, a well-known result from the paper and its follow-ups]: minimizers of the piecewise-smooth M–S can have **crack-tips**, where K ends inside Ω, so K need not enclose regions. The piecewise-constant case gives partitions (closed boundaries, with triple junctions at 120°).

**Ambrosio–Tortorelli 1990 (phase-field approximation)** [V]
- L. Ambrosio, V. M. Tortorelli, "Approximation of functionals depending on jumps by elliptic functionals via Γ-convergence," Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 43:999–1036, 1990 — [UniPi record](https://arpi.unipi.it/handle/11568/14155); described in [arXiv 2102.09872](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.09872)
- The binary set K is replaced by a smooth edge field s ∈ [0,1]: E_ε(u, s) = ∫ (u − f)² + μ ∫ s²|∇u|² + ν ∫ (ε|∇s|² + (1 − s)²/(4ε)). As ε → 0 this Γ-converges to M–S. s is near 0 near K and near 1 elsewhere, and the transition band shrinks with ε. — [UniPi record / summary](https://arpi.unipi.it/handle/11568/14155)
- On a graph, s lives on edges or nodes. The ε|∇s|² term spatially couples the edge field (diffuses it), which favours continuous edge curves. It is a soft remedy for fragmented breaks. Solvers alternate between u and s; each subproblem is a convex quadratic (linear solve). [U for the solver detail]

**Black & Rangarajan 1996 (line process ⇔ robust M-estimator)** [V-partial: DOI not confirmed]
- M. J. Black, A. Rangarajan, "On the unification of line processes, outlier rejection, and robust statistics with applications in early vision," IJCV 19(1):57–92, 1996 — [MPI-IS page](https://ps.is.tuebingen.mpg.de/publications/black-ijcv-1996); [Brown page](https://cs.brown.edu/people/mjblack/lineProc.html). Precursor: CVPR 1994 "The outlier process," DOI 10.1109/CVPR.1994.323805 — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/1994/black1994cvpr-outlier)
- The binary line process is generalized to an analog "outlier process." The paper gives a general method to convert a robust error norm into an outlier-process formulation and catalogs common norms with their equivalents. Applications: surface reconstruction, segmentation, optical flow. — [MPI-IS page](https://ps.is.tuebingen.mpg.de/publications/black-ijcv-1996)
- Core identity (the Black–Rangarajan duality, [U for exact conditions]): for a robust ρ with ρ(√t) concave in t, ρ(x) = min_(0≤l≤1) [ l x² + Ψ(l) ]. So any robust penalty (Geman–McClure, Lorentzian, Tukey, truncated quadratic) is a soft line process. A dedicated line-continuity prior (as in Geman & Geman) can then be added to l. — [Black–Rangarajan duality note](https://www.ce.unipr.it/~medici/geometry_en/node95.html)
- Practical consequence: IRLS / half-quadratic minimization means alternating (weights l_ij from the current residuals, then a weighted-least-squares solve for u). This is exactly an iterative "springs weaken/break, re-equilibrate" scheme, and GNC can be applied by scheduling the scale of ρ.

**Potts model / graph cuts (Boykov, Veksler, Zabih 2001)** [V-partial: PAMI DOI not confirmed]
- Y. Boykov, O. Veksler, R. Zabih, "Fast Approximate Energy Minimization via Graph Cuts," IEEE TPAMI 23(11):1222–1239, 2001 — [author abstract](https://www.csd.uwo.ca/~yboykov/Abstracts/pami01-abs.shtml); [PDF](https://www.csd.uwo.ca/~yboykov/Papers/pami01.pdf). ICCV 1999 version DOI 10.1109/ICCV.1999.791245 — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iccv/1999/boykov1999iccv-fast)
- Two move algorithms. α-β swap works for semimetric smoothness terms. α-expansion needs a metric and returns a local minimum with no improving expansion move, within a known factor of the global minimum. Global minimization is NP-hard even in the simplest discontinuity-preserving case (Potts). — [author abstract](https://www.csd.uwo.ca/~yboykov/Abstracts/pami01-abs.shtml)
- [U] The truncated quadratic min(K, |α−β|²) is only a semimetric, so expansion does not apply directly; swap or truncated-convex range moves (Veksler 2007, [U]) are used instead. The truncated L1 min(K, |α−β|) is a metric. For baillarger: if u is quantized to depth labels, a truncated-L1 spring is directly α-expansion-compatible.
- Labels give closed boundaries by construction: a boundary edge is any edge whose endpoints have different labels.

**Convex relaxations** [V for Pock et al. 2009 ICCV; U for the minimal-partition paper]
- T. Pock, D. Cremers, H. Bischof, A. Chambolle, "An Algorithm for Minimizing the Mumford-Shah Functional," ICCV 2009, pp. 1133–1140, DOI 10.1109/ICCV.2009.5459348 — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iccv/2009/pock2009iccv-algorithm); [PDF](https://cvai.cit.tum.de/_media/spezial/bib/pock_et_al_iccv09.pdf)
- The full (piecewise-smooth) M–S functional is relaxed to a convex problem by **functional lifting** (adding a range dimension) and solved with a primal-dual projection algorithm with proven convergence. The authors say it is the first convex-relaxation method for full M–S, so results do not depend on initialization. — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iccv/2009/pock2009iccv-algorithm). Caveat [U]: the relaxation is not tight in general, so thresholding the lifted solution can be suboptimal, and memory grows with the number of range levels. That is costly for d-dimensional laminar vectors.
- Real-time non-convex alternative: Strekalovskiy & Cremers, "Real-Time Minimization of the Piecewise Smooth Mumford-Shah Functional," ECCV 2014 — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/eccv/2014/strekalovskiy2014eccv-real) [V for title/venue only]. It uses a truncated quadratic directly with primal-dual iterations.
- [U] Pock, Chambolle, Cremers, Bischof, "A convex relaxation approach for computing minimal partitions," CVPR 2009. Convex relaxation of the Potts / minimal-partition problem. Check before citing.

**Potts on graphs / vector-valued data (Storath & Weinmann; cut pursuit)**
- M. Storath, A. Weinmann, "Fast Partitioning of Vector-Valued Images," SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 7(3):1826–1852, 2014, DOI 10.1137/130950367 [V] — [SIAM](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/130950367); [EPFL BIG](https://bigwww.epfl.ch/publications/storath1402.html). The Potts (piecewise-constant M–S) problem is solved by ADMM-type splitting into 1D Potts subproblems, each solved exactly by dynamic programming. The authors report it as much faster than graph cuts or convex relaxation for vector-valued data, with cost linear in the data dimension and no label discretization. — [EPFL BIG](https://bigwww.epfl.ch/publications/storath1402.html). The splitting is along lattice directions, so it does not transfer directly to an irregular cortical graph [inference].
- Weinmann, Storath et al., "Mumford-Shah and Potts Regularization for Manifold-Valued Data with Applications to DTI and Q-Ball Imaging" — [arXiv 1410.1699](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.1699) [V title only]. This handles values on manifolds, which would apply if laminar profiles were treated as normalized shapes.
- L. Landrieu, G. Obozinski, "Cut Pursuit: Fast Algorithms to Learn Piecewise Constant Functions on General Weighted Graphs," SIAM J. A working-set method that recursively splits level sets with graph cuts. It handles weighted-graph TV and its L0 counterpart (the M–S boundary size / Potts on graphs), and is fastest when the solution has few level sets. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v51/landrieu16.html). **This is the most directly usable solver for "Potts on the column graph" with vector-valued f_i.** The Python and C++ code is open source.

### Inferences
- **Closed boundaries by construction:** Potts / piecewise-constant M–S (any solver: α-expansion, Storath–Weinmann, cut pursuit, minimal-partition convex relaxations); multicut / correlation clustering ([U], e.g., Andres et al.); Ward / any clustering. **Not by construction:** line processes without a continuity prior, weak membrane / truncated quadratic on continuous u, piecewise-smooth M–S (crack-tips), Ambrosio–Tortorelli (soft edge field), robust IRLS.
- A cheap way to restore closure in an edge-based scheme on a graph: define areas as **connected components of the graph after removing broken edges**. Dangling cracks then do not separate anything and are harmless or can be pruned. This is equivalent to post-hoc projection onto a partition. The cost: an energy that counted the dangling cracks no longer matches the partition. The consistent fix is to optimize a partition objective (Potts / multicut) directly, in which the penalty is charged only for edges between different components.
- Truncated quadratic vs. Potts for baillarger. Truncated quadratic (weak membrane) lets laminar depths vary smoothly *within* an area (true cortex: thickness and curvature gradients) and breaks only at jumps. Piecewise-constant Potts forces flat laminar depths per area, so it will over-segment along smooth gradients (staircasing) unless f is first normalized, for example to relative depth or residuals after smooth detrending. A middle path [inference]: piecewise-smooth M–S whose discontinuity set is constrained to be a partition boundary. In practice: Potts on labels, plus a smooth model fit per region, iterated (the alternating "region-based M–S" of Chan–Vese / Zhu–Yuille type, [U]).
- **Order dependence of a cascading break.** If springs are broken greedily one at a time (break the most-stretched, re-equilibrate, repeat), the result depends on order and tie-breaking, because the energy is non-convex and each break redistributes strain to neighbours. That is the same cascade the user describes. Remedies: (a) minimize a single global energy jointly; (b) GNC continuation (anneal α or the robust scale from large to small, so springs break gradually and simultaneously); (c) a convex relaxation (Pock et al.) for initialization independence; (d) graph-cut moves, which change many labels at once rather than single sites (the reason α-expansion beats ICM); (e) report stability across random orders or bootstraps.
- Spring-breaking threshold: in the weak membrane the break threshold on |u_i − u_j| is √α/λ. The per-edge "cost of a break" is α, and summed over the boundary it is α × boundary length, so α directly trades off area size. Edge weights w_ij should include the inter-column distance, so that boundary length is approximated in mm rather than edge count (otherwise mesh anisotropy produces metrication artefacts [U]).

## Key Question 2: Piecewise-smooth surface fitting with discontinuities on graphs/meshes (TV vs truncated quadratic, L0 gradient minimization, Potts on graphs)

### Takeaway
On graphs, the penalties form a sequence: quadratic (no discontinuities, blurs borders), TV / L1 (convex, preserves jumps but shrinks contrast and staircases smooth gradients), robust / truncated quadratic (non-convex, piecewise-smooth with true breaks), L0 / Potts (non-convex, piecewise-constant, closed partitions). Solvers on irregular graphs exist for all of them, e.g., cut pursuit for TV and L0 on general weighted graphs, and L0 gradient minimization by half-quadratic splitting (originally on image grids, with mesh variants).

### Cited Findings
- L. Xu, C. Lu, Y. Xu, J. Jia, "Image Smoothing via L0 Gradient Minimization," ACM Trans. Graph. 30(6), Article 174 (SIGGRAPH Asia 2011), DOI 10.1145/2024156.2024208 [V; the project page lists issue 5, the authors' PDF lists 6] — [author PDF](https://www.lxu.me/mypapers/L0smooth_Siggraph_Asia2011.pdf); [project page](https://www.cse.cuhk.edu.hk/~leojia/projects/L0smoothing/)
  - Objective [standard, U for exact notation]: min_S Σ (S_p − I_p)² + λ · C(S), with C(S) = #{p : |∂_x S_p| + |∂_y S_p| ≠ 0}. Solved by half-quadratic splitting: auxiliary gradients h, v with a penalty β‖(h, v) − ∇S‖² and β increased geometrically. The (h, v) subproblem is a closed-form hard threshold (keep the gradient if its squared magnitude exceeds λ/β). The S subproblem is a linear (FFT) solve. The increasing β is itself a continuation scheme, like GNC.
  - Note: L0-gradient counts nonzero gradients *per pixel*, not per edge pair. It is close to Potts but does not explicitly produce labels, so tiny residual gradients and non-closed edges are possible [inference].
- Cut pursuit handles both graph TV, Σ w_ij|u_i − u_j|, and the L0 / Potts graph penalty, Σ w_ij 1[u_i ≠ u_j], on general weighted graphs, with a recursive graph-cut splitting of level sets — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v51/landrieu16.html). An extension to non-differentiable data terms with convergence proofs: Raguet & Landrieu — [arXiv 1802.04383](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.04383).
- Storath–Weinmann Potts (vector-valued, linear in dimension) — [SIAM](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/130950367); manifold-valued M–S/Potts — [arXiv 1410.1699](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.1699).
- Pock et al. 2009 report that the convex M–S relaxation yields smooth approximations that still preserve discontinuities — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iccv/2009/pock2009iccv-algorithm). Strekalovskiy & Cremers 2014 give a real-time piecewise-smooth M–S minimizer — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/eccv/2014/strekalovskiy2014eccv-real).
- Black & Rangarajan applied their outlier-process framework explicitly to surface reconstruction — [MPI-IS](https://ps.is.tuebingen.mpg.de/publications/black-ijcv-1996).

### Inferences
- For vector u_i (d laminar boundaries), the choice of norm in the spring matters. ρ(‖u_i − u_j‖₂) (group / isotropic) breaks all laminae together, which models an areal border. Per-component ρ(u_i^k − u_j^k) lets one lamina break alone, which models a local laminar anomaly or a segmentation error in one band. A border criterion could require coincident breaks in ≥ m bands [inference; matches Schleicher's multivariate profile-distance idea below].
- TV is attractive as a convex, initialization-free first pass (the cut-pursuit TV solution gives candidate regions). Then L0 / Potts or truncated-quadratic refinement, warm-started from it, is a practical GNC-like pipeline.
- On meshes, weight edges by length or cotangent weights so that the boundary penalty approximates geodesic length; otherwise the vertex-graph topology biases border orientation [U / inference].

## Key Question 3: Cortical parcellation, edge-based vs region-based vs hybrid (Wig 2014, Gordon 2016, Glasser 2016, Schaefer 2018 gwMRF, Schleicher's observer-independent method), and relevance to laminar profiles

### Takeaway
Cortical parcellation has followed both traditions. **Edge-based**: Schleicher et al. 1999 (multivariate profile-distance borders in histology), Wig 2014 / Gordon 2016 (RSFC gradient boundary maps then watershed), Glasser 2016 (gradient-defined borders with semi-automated delineation). **Region-based**: clustering (Ward, spectral, k-means; Craddock, Thirion [U]). **Hybrid**: Schaefer 2018 gwMRF, an MRF that combines a global similarity (clustering) data term with a pairwise Potts term weighted by local gradients. Watershed turns gradient maps into closed regions; gwMRF does so by being a labeling. Schleicher's method is closest to the laminar-profile setting, because it looks for sliding-window jumps in a multivariate distance between laminar intensity profiles.

### Cited Findings

**Schleicher et al. 1999, observer-independent border detection** [V]
- A. Schleicher, K. Amunts, S. Geyer, P. Morosan, K. Zilles, "Observer-independent method for microstructural parcellation of cerebral cortex: a quantitative approach to cytoarchitectonics," NeuroImage 9:165–177, 1999, DOI 10.1006/nimg.1998.0385 — citation as given in [Crossref-indexed reference list](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1007%2F11566465_37)
- Method (from secondary descriptions): gray-level index (GLI) profiles are sampled along traverses from pia to white matter. Ten shape features (central moments) are computed per profile. Borders are placed where the multivariate Mahalanobis distance between adjacent blocks of profiles changes significantly in shape — [PMC figure/description](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3429851/figure/F3). The 1998 Acta Stereologica precursor reported that Mahalanobis distance was more sensitive than Euclidean, and that the method confirmed visually defined borders in occipital cortex and found new ones — [Acta Stereologica](https://popups.uliege.be/0351-580x/index.php?id=2499)
- [U, standard description] The procedure: sliding windows of b profiles on each side of a position, distance D² between the two windows' mean feature vectors, significance by permutation (Hotelling T²), with a border accepted when it is a significant local maximum stable across several window sizes b. The method works along 1D sequences of profiles in a section, so closure into 2D areas comes from stacking sections or later 3D mapping, not from the method itself.

**Wig et al. 2014, RSFC boundary mapping** [V]
- G. S. Wig, T. O. Laumann, S. E. Petersen, "An approach for parcellating human cortical areas using resting-state correlations," NeuroImage 93 Pt 2:276–291, 2014 (online 2013) — [PMC3912214](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3912214); [WUSTL profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/an-approach-for-parcellating-human-cortical-areas-using-resting-s/)
- Abrupt transitions in RSFC patterns mark putative areal boundaries. Boundary locations replicated across independent subject groups and agreed with task and cytoarchitectonic data in many places — [WUSTL profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/an-approach-for-parcellating-human-cortical-areas-using-resting-s/). **Gordon et al. 2016, boundary map → watershed parcels** [V]
- E. M. Gordon, T. O. Laumann, B. Adeyemo, J. F. Huckins, W. M. Kelley, S. E. Petersen, "Generation and Evaluation of a Cortical Area Parcellation from Resting-State Correlations," Cerebral Cortex 26(1):288–303, 2016 (online Oct 2014), DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhu239 — [PMC4677978](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4677978)
- Pipeline (from full text): per-vertex connectivity maps → similarity matrix → spatial gradient on the closed surface (Workbench cifti-gradient) → averaged across subjects. "Watershed by flooding" replaced Wig's non-maxima suppression, and the boundary maps were averaged into a boundary-frequency map. Local minima of that map seeded a second watershed, producing >1,000 parcels with 1-vertex borders. Adjacent parcels were **merged if the median boundary value along their shared border was below a threshold** (60th percentile, chosen by visual inspection). Top-quartile boundary vertices were removed as transition zones, and low-SNR or <15-vertex parcels were dropped. Result: 422 parcels, 356 kept — [PMC4677978](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4677978)
- Stated limitations: the threshold was chosen visually; possible over-parcellation (e.g., somatomotor); some areas mis- or under-parcellated (e.g., hOc5); the result is "a purely functional parcellation" — [PMC4677978](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4677978)

**Glasser et al. 2016, HCP multimodal parcellation** [V]

**Schaefer et al. 2018, gradient-weighted MRF (hybrid)** [V]
- A. Schaefer, R. Kong, E. M. Gordon, T. O. Laumann, X.-N. Zuo, A. J. Holmes, S. B. Eickhoff, B. T. T. Yeo, "Local-Global Parcellation of the Human Cerebral Cortex from Intrinsic Functional Connectivity MRI," Cerebral Cortex 28:3095–3114, 2018, DOI 10.1093/cercor/bhx179 — [author PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/ythomas/publications/2018LocalGlobal-CerebCor.pdf); [bioRxiv 10.1101/135632](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/135632v2)
- gwMRF integrates local-gradient approaches (detect abrupt changes between neighbours) with global-similarity approaches (cluster similar fingerprints regardless of distance). Parcels were more homogeneous than four previously published parcellations and agreed with some histological and visuotopic boundaries. Released at 400–1000 parcels — [author PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/ythomas/publications/2018LocalGlobal-CerebCor.pdf)
- Energy structure [U, my reading of the paper; verify against the PDF]: E(l) = Σ_v −log p(x_v | μ_(l_v)) (a von Mises–Fisher global-similarity term on normalized fingerprints) + c Σ_(u~v) w_uv(G) · 1[l_u ≠ l_v] (a Potts term whose weight w decreases with the local gradient G between u and v, e.g., exp(−k·G), so cutting is cheap where gradients are high) + a spatial-connectedness / compactness term (a distance-to-centroid penalty keeps parcels spatially connected). Optimized by alternating graph-cut label updates and parameter (μ) updates. Closure comes by construction from labels.

### Inferences
- **Mapping to baillarger:** the current Ward merging is region-based. The proposed spring-breaking is edge-based (a Wig/Gordon-style boundary map, but computed on laminar depth differences, so in Schleicher's spirit). Gordon's "merge adjacent watershed basins if the median boundary strength along the shared border is low" is effectively a **hybrid of the two**. It is also the standard remedy for unclosed edge maps: watershed gives closed basins, then merging by border strength removes weak borders. This is a ready-made template: (1) per-edge discontinuity score d_ij (laminar Mahalanobis distance, Schleicher-like); (2) watershed or connected components on the graph; (3) region adjacency graph merging by median d_ij on shared borders, which can be combined with Ward's within-region variance (a gwMRF-like energy).
- A gwMRF analogue for laminar data: data term = within-parcel Gaussian likelihood of laminar depth vectors (or residuals after a smooth per-parcel fit, to allow thickness gradients); pairwise term = Potts with weight exp(−k · d_ij), where d_ij is the laminar discontinuity between columns. Solve with α-expansion or cut pursuit. This makes the springs a soft prior inside a closed-partition model, and it removes the order dependence of a greedy cascade.
- Schleicher's significance-testing and multi-window stability is a principled way to set the spring-breaking threshold (permutation null on D²) instead of a visual threshold (which was Gordon's limitation).

## Key Question 4: "Fault" detection analogous to laminar discontinuity (seismic horizons)

### Takeaway
Seismic interpretation addresses the same problem: layered reflectors (horizons) are continuous except at faults where they are offset. Mature methods (Hale 2013; Wu & Hale, Colorado School of Mines) first compute a **fault-likelihood** image, then extract fault surfaces as ridges, estimate throw by matching across the fault, and then "unfault" and flatten. Horizon trackers block propagation across faults, which is a hard line process. The cortical analogue: compute a laminar discontinuity likelihood per edge, extract borders as ridges, and allow a laminar "throw" (offset) across borders when flattening.

### Cited Findings
- D. Hale, "Methods to compute fault images, extract fault surfaces, and estimate fault throws from 3D seismic images," Geophysics 78(2):O33–O43, 2013 [V-partial: bibliographic details from a citing reference] — [CSEG summary](https://old.cseg.ca/technical/view/3d-seismic-image-processing-for-faults-and-horizons); [SEG wiki: Dave Hale](https://wiki.seg.org/wiki/David_Hale). It computes fault likelihood, strike, and dip images, and extracts fault surfaces as quadrilateral meshes following ridges of fault likelihood. Throws are estimated by comparing samples on opposite sides of the fault, which enables "unfaulting" — [CSEG summary](https://old.cseg.ca/technical/view/3d-seismic-image-processing-for-faults-and-horizons)
- X. Wu, PhD thesis, Colorado School of Mines (adviser D. Hale), 2016: automatic extraction of all fault, unconformity, and horizon surfaces. Unfaulted images with continuous reflectors across faults and unconformities constrain the automatic flattening. Horizons are taken as slices in flattened space and mapped back. Processing is by solving PDEs — [Mines repository](https://repository.mines.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/42c3e45c-3b46-4f70-b6df-c9270302bf57/content). The journal version (Wu & Hale, "Automatically interpreting all faults, unconformities, and horizons from 3D seismic images," Interpretation 2016) was not located [U].
- A 2015 EAGE abstract describes horizon patches that terminate at fault boundaries and are matched across fault planes — [EarthDoc](https://www.earthdoc.org/content/papers/10.3997/2214-4609.201412612) (authors not seen). A USPTO patent sets the horizon-propagation confidence to zero along block-bounding faults to stop propagation across them, a hard line process — [USPTO 9182511](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9182511)

### Inferences
- In seismic work, fault surfaces are extracted as ridges of a likelihood image, often as open surfaces with tips, because faults do terminate in rock. That is geologically correct there but wrong for areal borders, which must close. So the seismic analogy supports the *detection* stage (likelihood → ridges → throw), but the cortical *partition* stage needs closure (watershed + merge, Potts, gwMRF).
- "Throw" maps onto a laminar offset vector Δ = u_i − u_j across a border, a potentially useful border descriptor (which laminae jump, by how much).
- Flattening with faults (Wu & Hale) is analogous to fitting smooth laminar surfaces *per area* with discontinuities allowed only on the extracted border set. That is piecewise-smooth M–S with K restricted to closed partition boundaries (see Key Question 1 inferences).
