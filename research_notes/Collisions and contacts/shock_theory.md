# Shock / medial-axis theory for self-collisions of one front from a connected surface

Tags: [verified] = I read the statement in the cited source (the PDF text, or an abstract for abstract-level claims). [unverified] = from a search summary, from memory, or from a garbled text extraction that I could not read cleanly. [inference] = my own derivation or application to the cortex problem; not in a source.

Notation used below: M = the meeting set (shock set, medial locus) of the front grown from the WM surface S. r(x) = arrival time / distance of x. Spokes U_i = vectors from x to its contact (foot) points p_i on S. tau(p) = ribbon thickness (column length) at foot point p. alpha = object angle.

## 1. Generic local forms of the 3D medial axis (Giblin & Kimia) and the meaning of the A3 rim

### Takeaway
The generic 3D medial axis has exactly five local types: A1^2 sheets, A1^3 Y-junction curves, A3 rim (rib) curves, A1^4 six-junction points and A1A3 fin points. An A3 rim is where the two contact points of the sphere coalesce. The sphere there osculates S in the first principal direction (r = 1/kappa_1), and the contact point lies on a crest (ridge) of S. The rim is therefore the cusp edge of the focal surface of S, which is exactly a "focal point of the initial surface at distance = principal radius."

### Cited Findings
- [verified] A1^2 = centre of a sphere tangent to S at two distinct points ("bitangent sphere"); these points form sheets. A1^3 = sphere tangent at three distinct points; these form curves ("axes", "Y-junction curves"), each the intersection of three A1^2 sheets. A3 = two contact points come into coincidence; A3 points form "an A3 curve, also called a rim or rib curve, which locally bounds a single A1^2 sheet"; the contact points of these spheres "form a ridge or crest line on the surface". A1^4 = four contact points, at the intersection of six A1^2 sheets and four A1^3 curves ("6-junction points"). A1A3 ("fin point") = A1 contact at one point and A3 contact at another; both A1^3 curves and A3 rims end at A1A3 points. These five are "the only generic types of MA points in 3D." — [Giblin, Kimia & Pollitt, Transitions of the 3D medial axis under a one-parameter family of deformations](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/~pjgiblin/papers/paper8.pdf)
- [verified] The classification is by order of contact of the inscribed sphere with S. Transitions (non-generic events in one-parameter families, e.g. A1^5) are treated as collisions of these generic types; two A3 curves can meet only within the same sheet. — [Giblin, Kimia & Pollitt](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/~pjgiblin/papers/paper8.pdf)
- [verified, abstract] The hypergraph view: A1^3 curves either end at an A3 curve or meet three other A1^3 curves at an A1^4 point; nodes = A1^4 and A1A3 points, links = A1^3 and A3 curves, hyperlinks = A1^2 sheets. — [Giblin & Kimia, A formal classification of 3D medial axis points and their local geometry, CVPR 2000](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2000/giblin2000cvpr-formal) (journal version: IEEE PAMI 26(2):238–251, 2004 [unverified pages, from a search summary])
- [verified, abstract] Leymarie & Kimia's shock scaffold is the hierarchical organisation of the 3D medial axis into these special points and curves; it is computed directly from unorganised point clouds. — [Leymarie & Kimia, CVPR 2003](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2003/leymarie2003cvpr-computation)
- [unverified] Notation varies: some papers write sheets as A_1^2 and others as A_2^1. — [Chang & Kimia 3DIM 2009 slides](https://www.albany.edu/faculty/mchang2/files/2009_08_3DIM_Shock_Match.pdf)
- [verified] Pizer et al. list the conditions at the medial end-curve (A3) in 3D: the two boundary points coincide ("multiplicity 2"); the object angle is 0; kappa_1 = 1/r at the contact point; kappa_1 (with kappa_1 > kappa_2) is a relative maximum along its own line of curvature, i.e. the contact point is a crest point. The boundary is osculated there by the disk of radius 1/kappa_1 in the first principal direction, and "the end curve (point) of the medial axis is a cusp of the focal surface of the boundary." In 2D the end-point also has dr/ds = −1 (along the axis). — [Pizer, Siddiqi, Székely, Damon & Zucker, Multiscale medial loci and their properties, IJCV 55 (2003), §2.1.2, §2.2.3](https://mail.jndamon.sites.oasis.unc.edu/Multiscale.med.axis.pdf)
- [verified] At every Blum medial point, "1/r is no less than the larger principal curvature of the surface parallel to the boundary at locally constant distance r into the figure." This is the local condition that no focal point lies before the medial point along the spoke. — [Pizer et al. 2003 §2.2](https://mail.jndamon.sites.oasis.unc.edu/Multiscale.med.axis.pdf)
- [verified] Ridges (crests) are points where a principal curvature is extremal along its own principal direction. The derivative definition is sensitive to noise. — [Edelsbrunner et al., The mid-sphere cousin of the medial axis transform, arXiv 2504.14743](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.14743)

### Inferences
- [inference] For the cortex: an A3 rim of the WM-front meeting set lies at distance rho_1 = 1/kappa_1 from a crest line of the WM surface, on the side to which the WM is concave (as seen from the cortex). Such crest lines are typically sulcal fundi. The rim is inside the ribbon only if rho_1(crest) < tau at that crest. This gives a sourced, purely local test of whether a fold can make the front meet itself inside the ribbon: compare the WM focal radius on the crest with the thickness.
- [inference] The "two sides" of the meeting set coalesce only at A3 rims. Elsewhere on a sheet the two contact points are distinct, which makes "two sides" well-defined at every A1^2 point.
- [inference] The theory is Euclidean (inscribed spheres). For a data-steered arrival field (anisotropic / Riemannian metric), the analogues are the cut locus (A1^2-like: two minimising characteristics arrive) and the focal / conjugate locus (A3-like: endpoint of the cut locus where neighbouring characteristics cross). The classification by contact order does not transfer verbatim; the qualitative picture (sheets ending on focal edges) should transfer. See Gaps.

## 2. Local (focusing) vs global (apposition) shocks on a connected surface; how sheets are bounded

### Takeaway
No pointwise invariant on a sheet makes "fold" vs "contact" a discrete distinction: the sheet near an A3 rim continues smoothly into a sheet of apposed parts. That is the continuum the project found. Theory offers three sourced handles. (a) Damon: the radial (spoke) flow is locally a diffeomorphism when the radial curvature condition holds; the boundary fails to embed only through the "nonlocal intersection" of points from distant parts of M. (b) The object angle alpha / radius gradient, which runs from 0 at a rim to pi/2 between parallel apposed banks. (c) Rims are where the contact points merge. A discrete, generic classification exists for the birth of each sheet component rather than for its points: a component is born either at an A3 rim (focal, local) or at an interior critical point of r restricted to the sheet (two distant parts first touch, global) [inference, built on the Kimia shock orders, which are unverified].

### Cited Findings
- [verified] Generic sheet boundaries: an A3 curve "locally bounds a single A1^2 sheet"; A1^3 curves are where three sheets meet. Within the generic list there is no other way for a sheet to end in the interior of the region. — [Giblin, Kimia & Pollitt](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/~pjgiblin/papers/paper8.pdf)
- [verified] Damon's skeletal structures (M, U): U is the multivalued radial vector field from M to the tangency points, U = r·U_1. He defines the radial shape operator S_rad, its eigenvalues the "principal radial curvatures" kappa_ri, an edge shape operator S_E and a compatibility 1-form. The three conditions (radial curvature condition, edge condition, compatibility condition) are "(necessary and) sufficient" for the associated boundary B to be smooth. — [Damon, Smoothness and geometry of boundaries associated to skeletal structures II, Compositio Math. 2004, Introduction](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5306202B17E81E3892A290107AA2DAF0/S0010437X04000570a.pdf/smoothness-and-geometry-of-boundaries-associated-to-skeletal-structures-ii-geometry-in-the-blum-case.pdf)
- [verified] Radial curvature condition (stated in Corollary 5.6 for a diffeomorphic image): r < min 1/kappa_ri over all positive principal radial curvatures, at all non-edge points. The edge condition is the same with the generalised eigenvalues of the edge shape operator. — [Damon II, Cor. 5.6, eqs. 5.17–5.18](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5306202B17E81E3892A290107AA2DAF0/S0010437X04000570a.pdf/smoothness-and-geometry-of-boundaries-associated-to-skeletal-structures-ii-geometry-in-the-blum-case.pdf)
- [verified] Partial Blum case (U orthogonal to the boundary): the boundary shape operator is S_B = (I − r·S_v)^(-1) S_v. Principal curvatures are kappa_i = kappa_ri / (1 − r·kappa_ri), equivalently kappa_ri = kappa_i / (1 + r·kappa_i). The "radii of curvature equation" is r_ri = r + r_i, with r_i = 1/kappa_i and r_ri = 1/kappa_ri signed. Principal radial directions map to principal directions. At crest points corresponding to edge points of M, the boundary principal curvatures are limits of the eigenvalues of (I − r·S_E)^(-1) S_E. — [Damon II, Thm 3.2, eqs 3.3, 3.4, 3.6; Cor. 3.9](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5306202B17E81E3892A290107AA2DAF0/S0010437X04000570a.pdf/smoothness-and-geometry-of-boundaries-associated-to-skeletal-structures-ii-geometry-in-the-blum-case.pdf)
- [verified] "Partial Blum" requires only the compatibility condition (radial vector orthogonal to the boundary). The full Blum condition adds e.g. equal spoke lengths at smooth points and a spoke difference orthogonal to M. — [Damon II, Introduction](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5306202B17E81E3892A290107AA2DAF0/S0010437X04000570a.pdf/smoothness-and-geometry-of-boundaries-associated-to-skeletal-structures-ii-geometry-in-the-blum-case.pdf)
- [unverified: garbled text extraction] Damon I (p. 1945) states that under the conditions the radial map is a local diffeomorphism. B "will only fail to be an embedded manifold due to the nonlocal intersection of points" corresponding to distant parts of M; if there are no such nonlocal intersections, B is an embedded submanifold. The local conditions therefore control local (focal) singularities, and global embedding is a separate, nonlocal requirement. — [Damon, Smoothness and geometry of boundaries associated to skeletal structures I, Ann. Inst. Fourier 53 (2003) 1941–1985](https://aif.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/aif.1997.pdf)
- [verified, abstract] Damon's global structure theorem decomposes the 3D Blum medial axis into "irreducible components" and represents each as surfaces with boundary attached to 4-valent graphs. — [Damon, The global medial structure of regions in R^3, Geom. Topol. 10 (2006)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.0394)
- [verified, abstract] Skeletal linking structures for multi-region configurations extend each region's skeleton. A generic configuration admits a "Blum" linking structure (proved by transversality of "multi-distance" and "height-distance" functions). — [Damon & Gasparovic, arXiv 1402.5517](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.5517)
- [unverified, memory: Kimia, Tannenbaum & Zucker, "Shapes, shocks, and deformations I", IJCV 1995; not retrieved] The 2D shocks are classified by the radius (arrival-time) function along the shock. First-order: r monotone along the branch, born at a curvature extremum (A3), "protrusion". Second-order: interior local minimum of r, where two separate boundary parts first meet and the branch then grows both ways, "neck". Third-order: r constant, parallel boundary parts, "bend". Fourth-order: interior local maximum of r, where the front annihilates, "seed".

### Inferences
- [inference] Morse-type birth classification (main proposal). Sweep the arrival time t upward and watch the components of M ∩ {r ≤ t}. Generically a new component is born in one of two ways. (i) At a rim point where r restricted to the A3 curve is minimal: the two foot points coincide (alpha = 0, |grad_M r| = 1). This is a focal (local) birth. (ii) At an interior point of an A1^2 sheet where r restricted to M has a local minimum: grad_M r = 0, so alpha = pi/2 and the spokes are antiparallel. These are two distinct, separated parts of S touching for the first time: a global birth (the 3D "neck" / second-order shock). Each birth event is discrete, unlike the continuous foot-point distance. This is the principled local–global distinction for a front from a connected surface.
- [inference] After birth, sheets from the two kinds of events can grow into each other through A1^3 junctions, or the same sheet can carry both regimes. A sulcus is the standard example: the sheet is born at the fundus rim (if rho_1 < tau) and continues upward between the banks, where alpha → pi/2 and r → tau. A per-sheet label is therefore not enough, and the per-point test must use r vs tau as well (see §3).
- [inference] Mapping to the project's two categories:
  - Contact (two pial surfaces apposed): points with r ≈ tau(p_1) ≈ tau(p_2), alpha ≈ pi/2 (antiparallel spokes), |grad_M r| ≈ 0. Typically the sheet component's birth is a global (neck) event at t ≈ tau, or the sheet is reached from a rim-born region only once r ≥ ~tau.
  - Fold tighter than ribbon (front focuses inside the ribbon): points with r < tau on a component whose r-descending path along M ends on an A3 rim with r_rim = rho_1(crest) < tau. Here alpha < pi/2 and it decreases to 0 toward the rim.
  - A third case the theory predicts: global births at t < tau (interior minimum of r on M with r < tau). Two separated WM regions are closer than 2·tau, so the ribbons physically overlap: a fused or under-resolved sulcus, or a segmentation error. Flag these rather than force them into either class.
- [inference] Sign of focusing: a bank produces a rim on the cortex side only where the WM surface is concave toward the cortex (sulcal fundi). Gyral crowns defocus and give no rims in the ribbon. With signed curvature positive for focusing, the Blum/Pizer condition r ≤ 1/kappa_1 along each spoke can be written as a "focal ratio" q_i = r·kappa_1(p_i) ∈ [0, 1], with q = 1 at a rim. The Damon radii equation r_ri = r + r_i is the exact sourced form of this. The sign convention must be checked against Damon's definitions before coding.
- [inference] Is every A1^2 sheet bounded by A3 rims or the domain boundary? No. Generic sheets are bounded by A3 rims and/or A1^3 junction curves (and pass through A1^4 / A1A3 points). In our truncated problem, sheets are also cut by the stopping surface r = tau (the pial surface). A sheet born at a neck can be bounded only by junctions and the truncation, with no rim at all.

## 3. Computable skeleton tools: flux, Hamilton–Jacobi skeletons, shock scaffold, radial shape operator

### Takeaway
Average outward flux (AOF) of grad D through a shrinking neighbourhood detects medial points and also measures the object angle: in 2D it is −(2/pi)·sin(alpha) at sheet (regular) points, −(1/pi)(sin alpha − alpha) at end points, and −(1/pi)·Σ sin(alpha_i) at junctions. With cos(2·alpha) = n_1·n_2, the flux magnitude is a direct, local, sourced measure of where a point sits on the rim (alpha = 0) to apposition (alpha = pi/2) continuum.

### Cited Findings
- [verified] Object angle: with n_1, n_2 the unit inward normals at the two contact points and t_P the unit skeleton tangent, alpha(P) ∈ [0, pi/2] is defined by n_1·n_2 = cos(2·alpha(P)), and n_i·t_P = cos(alpha(P)). — [Dimitrov, Damon & Siddiqi, Flux invariants for shape, CVPR 2003](https://mail.jndamon.sites.oasis.unc.edu/CVPR.pap.rev.pdf)
- [verified] 2D limits of the AOF of grad D over shrinking circles, lim F_eps(P)/(2·pi·eps): regular points −(2/pi)·sin(alpha); end points −(1/pi)(sin(alpha) − alpha); junction points −(1/pi)·Σ_{i=1..n} sin(alpha_i); non-skeletal points 0. (The plain outward flux limit is zero for all points; only the average flux separates them.) — [Dimitrov, Damon & Siddiqi, Table 1](https://mail.jndamon.sites.oasis.unc.edu/CVPR.pap.rev.pdf)
- [verified] The boundary can be reconstructed from skeleton, radius and object angle: C_{1,2}(t) = S(t) + r(t)·R(±alpha(t))·S'(t). In experiments they chose skeleton end points by an object-angle threshold (above 30°). — [Dimitrov, Damon & Siddiqi §3.5, §4](https://mail.jndamon.sites.oasis.unc.edu/CVPR.pap.rev.pdf)
- [verified, abstract] Hamilton–Jacobi skeletons: the AOF of the Hamiltonian (grassfire) vector field has very different limits at singular vs non-singular points. This drives a homotopy-preserving thinning. — [Siddiqi, Bouix, Tannenbaum & Zucker, IJCV 48 (2002)](https://www.sci.utah.edu/~gerig/CS7960-S2010/handouts/Hamilton-Jacobi-Skeleton-Siddiqi-Bouix.pdf); implementation details in [He, Kang & Álvarez, IPOL 2021](https://www.ipol.im/pub/art/2021/296/)
- [verified] Blum's branch-importance measure is the speed of skeleton formation as fire fronts meet. It goes to infinity as the opposite boundary segments become parallel, and Blum regarded a branch as more important the more parallel its boundary segments are. — [Pizer et al. 2003 §2.3.1](https://mail.jndamon.sites.oasis.unc.edu/Multiscale.med.axis.pdf). (The extracted text gives speed ∝ 1/sin of a half-angle, with the angle symbol garbled. I could not confirm which angle convention is meant.)
- [verified] Damon's S_rad, kappa_ri, the radial curvature condition and S_B = (I − r·S_v)^(-1)·S_v (see §2). — [Damon II](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5306202B17E81E3892A290107AA2DAF0/S0010437X04000570a.pdf/smoothness-and-geometry-of-boundaries-associated-to-skeletal-structures-ii-geometry-in-the-blum-case.pdf)

### Inferences
- [inference] My derivation from the Dimitrov definition (Euclidean, unit-speed front): the meeting point moves along the sheet tangent t at speed v with v·(n_i·t) = 1, so v = 1/cos(alpha). Equivalently, the magnitude of the radius gradient along the sheet is |grad_M r| = cos(alpha). Limits: alpha = 0 at a rim (|grad_M r| = 1, matching Pizer's dr/ds = −1 at end points) and alpha = pi/2 for apposed parallel banks (|grad_M r| = 0, infinite formation speed). In 3D the "sheet tangent" is the direction of grad_M r within the sheet.
- [inference] Practical estimators of alpha at a detected meeting voxel, from cheapest to most robust: (a) the angle between the two one-sided gradients of the arrival field T, cos(2·alpha) = −g_1·g_2/(|g_1||g_2|) with g_i the gradient of T on side i. The sign comes from spokes pointing back along −grad T; the convention must be checked. (b) The flux of grad T / |grad T| through a small sphere, compared with the regular-point value. The 3D constant is not sourced, so calibrate on a synthetic slab and cylinder. (c) |grad_M T| along the detected sheet.
- [inference] For a data-steered (anisotropic) T, use the metric-normalised characteristic directions (the directions in which the front actually moves) rather than the raw grad T. A divergence/flux test on raw grad T would mix anisotropy into alpha.
- [inference] The Damon formulas need S_rad on the sheet, which needs the second derivatives of the sheet and of r. Noisier than alpha. The useful special case is the radii equation r_ri = r + r_i, applied along each spoke, which gives the focal ratio test of §2 from the WM surface's own principal curvatures without differentiating the shock sheet.

## 4. Pruning / significance measures and their use for contact vs fold

### Takeaway
Local significance measures (object angle, Delaunay-triangle pointedness, formation speed) are functions of alpha. They separate rim-adjacent (curvature-induced) sheet regions from apposition regions but cannot be thresholded into a clean class, because alpha varies continuously along one sheet. Global measures (erosion thickness, lambda-medial axis) are more stable to noise. The lambda-medial axis criterion is, up to scale, the object angle again: the half-chord between the foot points is r·sin(alpha) [inference].

### Cited Findings
- [verified] Local significance measures for pruned Voronoi skeletons estimate the object angle from the medial atom and its generating points, or use Delaunay-triangle "pointedness", or the directional behaviour of the distance function across the ridge. Simple thresholding does not guarantee homotopy equivalence; topology preservation needs explicit checks; in 3D the processing-order problem is "mostly unsolved". Local measures can rate globally insignificant perturbations highly. — [Pizer et al. 2003 §4.2](https://mail.jndamon.sites.oasis.unc.edu/Multiscale.med.axis.pdf)
- [verified] In 3D there is no unique topological skeleton and no induced hierarchy between skeletal sheets, so no well-defined partial order of sheets as in 2D. Deleting one constituent can change the topological relations of others. — [Pizer et al. 2003 §2.3.2](https://mail.jndamon.sites.oasis.unc.edu/Multiscale.med.axis.pdf)
- [verified, abstract] The lambda-medial axis (Chazal & Lieutier) is a subset of the medial axis that captures the homotopy type of the complement for lambda ≤ wfs (weak feature size). For "regular" lambda it is stable under Hausdorff perturbation. — [Chazal & Lieutier, SMA 2004](https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/sm20041396/243-248_chazal.pdf)
- [unverified, from memory, not in the retrieved abstract] Definition: the lambda-medial axis keeps the points whose set of nearest boundary points has a smallest enclosing ball of radius ≥ lambda.
- [verified, abstract] The (lambda, alpha)-medial axis has Gromov–Hausdorff distance 1/4-Hölder and Hausdorff distance 1/2-Hölder in the Hausdorff distance between the input sets. — [Lieutier & Wintraecken, STOC 2023 / arXiv 2303.04014](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.04014)
- [verified, abstract] Erosion thickness (ET) is a global significance measure extended to 3D medial axes. It outperforms local measures in separating small boundary noise from prominent features and is faster than earlier global measures. — [Yan, Sykes, Chambers, Letscher & Ju, SIGGRAPH 2016](https://www.cs.wustl.edu/~taoju/research/et.pdf)

### Inferences
- [inference] For two foot points at distance r from x with spoke angle 2·alpha (spokes antiparallel at alpha = pi/2), the half-chord |p_1 − p_2|/2 = r·sin(alpha). This is the radius of the smallest enclosing ball of the two foot points. So the lambda-medial axis threshold is lambda ≤ r·sin(alpha), and a scale-free version (lambda/r) is just sin(alpha). Pruning near-rim sheet regions by lambda/r therefore removes the curvature-driven (focusing) part and keeps the apposition part. That is the right operation for "find contacts", but it is still a threshold on a continuum.
- [inference] Erosion thickness and similar global measures rank whole branches by how much shape they support, not by why they formed. They suit removing noise-induced sheets (bumps on a segmented WM surface create spurious rims at small r), not separating fold from contact. Suggested use: first remove sheet regions born at rims with tiny rho_1 (WM segmentation roughness, rho_1 of a few voxels or less; consistent with the project's minimum band size of 2–3 voxels), then classify the rest by the birth rule and r vs tau.
- [inference] Noise-induced shocks are exactly rim births at small r. The birth rule in §2 isolates them, and a minimum r_rim (in voxels) is a defensible prune parameter because it is a curvature radius of the WM surface, not a distance between banks.

## 5. Defining the two sides of a meeting sheet locally (spokes, local complementary components)

### Takeaway
Sourced: at every non-edge point of a skeletal structure, the radial field U is multivalued with one spoke into each "local complementary component" of the region near that point. The two sides are defined locally by the two spokes, and they are distinct at every A1^2 point and coalesce only at A3 rims. Whether the two spokes come from "nearby" or "distant" parts of S has no sharp pointwise answer. The sourced local quantity is alpha (or |grad_M r|); the discrete answer is the birth type of the sheet component (§2).

### Cited Findings
- [verified] A skeletal structure is (M, U): a Whitney-stratified set M with the multivalued radial vector field U from points of M to the corresponding tangency points on the boundary. The Blum medial axis is a special case. — [Damon II, Introduction](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5306202B17E81E3892A290107AA2DAF0/S0010437X04000570a.pdf/smoothness-and-geometry-of-boundaries-associated-to-skeletal-structures-ii-geometry-in-the-blum-case.pdf)
- [unverified: garbled extraction] Damon I defines, for a point x_0 of M, the "local complementary components" (connected components of the complement of M in a small neighbourhood of x_0) and "local neighbouring components" of M. U takes one value pointing into each local complementary component. The fin creation point (A1A3) has two complementary components, one above and one below M. — [Damon I, §1](https://aif.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/aif.1997.pdf)
- [verified] At an A1^2 point the two contact points are distinct, and they coincide at A3. — [Giblin, Kimia & Pollitt](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/~pjgiblin/papers/paper8.pdf)
- [verified] In 2D, an end point of a skeleton branch has a single boundary point of multiplicity 2 and object angle 0. — [Pizer et al. §2.1.2](https://mail.jndamon.sites.oasis.unc.edu/Multiscale.med.axis.pdf)

### Inferences
- [inference] A computable local side labelling without global labels: at a meeting voxel x, take the two one-sided characteristic directions (the upwind arrival directions from each side, equivalently the two values of grad T). Label each neighbouring voxel y by which of the two spokes its own back-traced characteristic is continuous with. In practice, use the sign of (y − x)·(g_1 − g_2), where g_i are the one-sided unit gradients, as a first-order proxy. That proxy is valid while alpha is not near 0; at alpha ≈ 0 (near a rim) g_1 ≈ g_2 and the sides become ill-defined, as the theory says they must.
