# How neuroimaging surface and thickness tools handle fused sulci, and whether they use thickness priors

Scope: how each tool places the pial surface or contact surface in fused ("kissing", buried, blurred) sulci; any thickness prior, maximum-thickness limit, or propagation from neighbouring cortex; probabilistic or uncertainty treatment; failure modes and how they were validated.

Verification legend: **[V]** = checked this session against a primary source page (PMC full text, tool docs, or the publisher or proceedings page). **[V-abs]** = checked against an abstract or search snippet only. **[U]** = from background knowledge, NOT checked this session; check before citing.

Research limits this session: the Dahnke et al. 2013 PBT full text (Manchester mirror) timed out twice, and the Elsevier and PNAS pages blocked fetches (403 or a redirect page), so the PBT and Fischl & Dale method details below are partly [U]. ~20 tool calls were used.

## (a) How the pial or contact surface is placed in fused sulci, tool by tool

### Takeaway
Every established tool uses one of three mechanisms to resolve fused banks: (1) a collision or skeleton barrier between fronts that grow from the two banks (FreeSurfer self-intersection prevention, CLASP, ACE, DELFMAP, PBT's WM-distance maxima); (2) diffeomorphic non-crossing (DiReCT); or (3) a learned prior (deep learning). In all of them, the meeting location comes from symmetric geometry (equal distance or equal arrival from WM, or a potential-field collision), modified only by whatever intensity evidence of CSF exists. None of them, as found here, scales a bank's speed by its own expected thickness to move the contact off the midline. The closest precedents are DiReCT's spatially varying thickness-prior image, which can make the banks asymmetric (an upper bound, not a target), and PBT's stated handling of "sulcal asymmetries".

### Cited Findings

**FreeSurfer (mris_make_surfaces / mris_place_surface; Fischl & Dale 2000)**
- Citation [V]: Fischl B, Dale AM. "Measuring the thickness of the human cerebral cortex from magnetic resonance images." PNAS 2000;97(20):11050–11055. PMID 10984517, PMCID PMC27146 — [DOI 10.1073/pnas.200033797](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.200033797) (metadata via PubMed; PMC full text not returned by the tool).
- Abstract [V-abs]: the intersubject SD of the thickness measures is < 0.5 mm. Reliability was tested by within-subject test–retest and by comparing regional thickness with published values — [PubMed/PMC27146](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC27146/).
- mris_make_surfaces places the tessellation at the white surface first, then deforms it to the gray/CSF (pial) surface. `-q` "Omit self-intersection and only generate gray/white surface", which implies that self-intersection handling is part of the default pial step. T2 options: `-T2_min`/`-T2_max` ("threshold for min/max T2 values allowed to be interior to the cortical ribbon"), `-nsigma_above`/`-nsigma_below`, `-wm_weight` (default 3), `-pa` pial curvature averaging (default 16) [V] — [FreeSurfer wiki: mris_make_surfaces](https://surfer.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/fswiki/mris_make_surfaces).
- The T2 or FLAIR refinement in recon-all keeps the original surfaces as `?h.woT2.pial` / `?h.woFLAIR.pial`. It is recommended with ~1 mm³ T2/FLAIR [V-abs] — [FreeSurfer mailing list](https://mail.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/archives/list/freesurfer@nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/thread/JV44EYLRRZLWUSE6ML4SYPTXXF6E2KCW/).
- Comparison by Osechinskiy & Kruggel [V]: FreeSurfer "corrects topological defects in the initial WM/GM surface and prevents mesh self-intersection", which they describe as costly and scaling poorly. FreeSurfer is tuned for 1 mm data and conforms the input to that voxel size — [Osechinskiy & Kruggel 2012, PMC3296314](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/).
- [U] Mechanism in buried sulci: the pial surface moves outward from the white surface along the normal. Self-intersection tests stop the opposing banks where they meet, so in a fully fused sulcus the two pial sheets come to rest close together near the midline of the GM between the two white surfaces. This is the "meeting in the middle" behaviour the user describes. I did not find a FreeSurfer document that states this explicitly.

**CAT12 projection-based thickness (PBT; Dahnke, Yotter & Gaser 2013)**
- Citation [V-abs]: Dahnke R, Yotter RA, Gaser C. "Cortical thickness and central surface estimation." NeuroImage 2013;65:336–348. [DOI 10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.09.050](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.09.050) — [Scinapse record](https://scinapse.io/papers/1999522048).
- PBT uses the tissue segmentation to estimate a WM distance, then projects local maxima onto the GM voxels, using the WM distance as the neighbour relationship. The paper states that PBT "allows the handling of partial volume information, sulcal blurring, and sulcal asymmetries without explicit sulcus reconstruction via skeleton or thinning methods" [V-abs, search snippet of abstract] — [search result pointing to the paper](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/fumie.costen/pastwork/DATA/readingmaterial/corticalthickness4.pdf).
- Motivation as quoted from the introduction [V-abs]: inside a sulcus "the CSF volume is small and affected by noise", which makes the outer boundary hard to define. Methods without sulcal modelling tend to overestimate thickness in blurred regions, or must work only on non-blurred gyral regions — [same](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/fumie.costen/pastwork/DATA/readingmaterial/corticalthickness4.pdf).
- The central surface (CS) is built from the thickness map, and the phantom validation "confirms accurate CS construction and cortical thickness measurement under a wide set of parameters for several thickness levels" [V-abs] — [PMC6174993 (citing paper)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6174993).
- [U] Details I believe are in the paper but could not fetch: (1) the WM distance (WMD) is computed through the GM. (2) In blurred sulci, the local maxima of the WMD (where the fronts from opposing banks meet) serve as the implicit sulcal midline. (3) The thickness at the outer boundary is propagated (projected) back along the WMD gradient to all GM voxels of the column, so that every voxel gets the column's full thickness. (4) In unblurred sulci a CSF distance is also used. (5) The CS is then taken at the 50% level of a percentage-position map. How "sulcal asymmetries" are handled (for example, whether the maximum may sit off the geometric midline) is the key open detail. **Recommendation: read Sections 2.x of the full text** — it is the closest precedent to the user's thickness-propagation idea.

**CRUISE / ACE (Han et al. 2004), MaCRUISE, Nighres**
- Citation [V-abs]: Han X, Pham DL, Tosun D, Rettmann ME, Xu C, Prince JL. "CRUISE: Cortical reconstruction using implicit surface evolution." NeuroImage 2004;23:997–1012 — [USUHS record](https://scholar.usuhs.edu/en/publications/cruise-cortical-reconstruction-using-implicit-surface-evolution/). Method: fuzzy classification, topology correction, and a topology-preserving geometric deformable model for the inner, central, and outer surfaces.
- ACE (Anatomically Consistent Enhancement) edits the GM membership: it builds a separating barrier from a skeleton of a weighted distance function, so that the outer surface can enter fused sulci. Osechinskiy & Kruggel report that ACE skeletons can have spurious branches and discontinuities, need a minimum-distance cutoff near WM, and degrade under strong noise. At 0.35 mm, CRUISE with default settings was much slower and did not give adequate pial results [V] — [PMC3296314](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/).
- Nighres `cruise_cortex_extraction` includes "Anatomically-Consistent Enhancement of sulcal fundi (ACE)" as a customized partial-voluming correction [V-abs] — [Nighres source](https://nighres.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/nighres/cortex/cruise_cortex_extraction.html).
- MaCRUISE / MaACE (Huo et al., NeuroImage 2016) [V-abs]: MaACE "is able to detect sulci in the outer surface that are not detected by ACE, particularly when CSF evidence is not visible". It uses multi-atlas segmentation as additional evidence — [PMC4927397](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4927397).
- Osechinskiy & Kruggel (SPIE 2011) proposed intrasulcal medial-surface identification as an alternative to ACE [V-abs] — [SPIE 7962, 79620N](https://spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/7962/79620N/Identifying-intrasulcal-medial-surfaces-for-anatomically-consistent-reconstruction-of-the/10.1117/12.877863.full).

**DELFMAP (Osechinskiy & Kruggel 2012)** [V]
- Citation: Osechinskiy S, Kruggel F. "Cortical Surface Reconstruction from High-Resolution MR Brain Images." Int J Biomed Imaging 2012;2012:870196. [DOI 10.1155/2012/870196](https://doi.org/10.1155/2012/870196), [PMC3296314](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/).
- Mechanism: an electrostatic-style potential field in which WM is a charged conductor and GM a dielectric whose permittivity follows the GM probability. Flow lines concentrate in GM and collide in sulci. Collisions in the correspondence functions plus shocks in the distance field mark a skeleton of the sulcal medial surface (correspondence test D_min = 4 voxels with 6 neighbours; shock threshold T = 0.8). The pial level set is advected along the field gradient, with the skeleton as a barrier and simple-point topology preservation. The collision location is therefore set by the field (symmetric for symmetric GM probability), not by a thickness expectation.

**CLASP (Kim et al. 2005) and ASP (MacDonald et al. 2000)**
- [V] Per Osechinskiy & Kruggel: CLASP uses a Laplacian map between the GM/WM interface and a CSF skeleton, with a self-proximity term to prevent self-intersection in sulci. Its accuracy can suffer where fused banks are not resolved, and the self-proximity cost may be prohibitive at high resolution — [PMC3296314](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/).
- [U] Citations: Kim JS et al. "Automated 3-D extraction and evaluation of the inner and outer cortical surfaces using a Laplacian map and partial volume effect classification." NeuroImage 2005;27(1):210–221. MacDonald D, Kabani N, Avis D, Evans AC. "Automated 3-D extraction of inner and outer surfaces of cerebral cortex from MRI." NeuroImage 2000;12(3):340–356. ASP deforms the inner and outer meshes together, with vertex-to-vertex links and a min/max inter-surface distance term (a thickness range constraint) plus a self-intersection penalty. The exact numeric range was not verified.

**Zeng et al. 1999 (coupled surfaces)** [U]
- Zeng X, Staib LH, Schultz RT, Duncan JS. "Segmentation and measurement of the cortex from 3-D MR images using coupled-surfaces propagation." IEEE TMI 1999;18(10):927–937. Two level sets (inner and outer) evolve together, with a coupling that keeps their distance within a plausible thickness band (I recall ~1–5 mm). This is a hard thickness range, not a per-region expectation, and it does not use intensity evidence to place the midline.

**ANTs DiReCT / KellyKapowski (Das et al. 2009)** [V]
- Citation: Das SR, Avants BB, Grossman M, Gee JC. "Registration based cortical thickness measurement." NeuroImage 2009;45(3):867–879 (online Dec 2008) — [PMC2836782](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836782).
- A diffeomorphic map from the GM/WM interface to the GM/CSF interface. Because the map is a diffeomorphism, the banks cannot cross and self-intersections "are automatically prevented". Closed sulci are resolved as infinitesimally narrow gaps. With no visible CSF, the banks come out symmetric, and thickness can be overestimated at the fundus where there is no depth information — [PMC2836782](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836782).

**BrainSuite** [U]
- Shattuck DW, Leahy RM. "BrainSuite: an automated cortical surface identification tool." Medical Image Analysis 2002;6(2):129–142. The pial surface is obtained by dilating the WM surface within the GM mask, with no explicit treatment of kissing sulci that I am aware of. Later BrainSuite versions use a "pial surface expansion" constrained by tissue fractions. Not verified this session.

**Laplace thickness (Jones et al. 2000)** [U]
- Jones SE, Buchbinder BR, Aharon I. "Three-dimensional mapping of cortical thickness using Laplace's equation." Human Brain Mapping 2000;11(1):12–32. Thickness is the length of streamlines of the Laplace solution between the inner and outer boundaries. It needs an outer boundary, so in fused sulci the result depends entirely on the upstream skeleton or CSF reconstruction. A missing skeleton gives streamlines that cross the fused region, which overestimates thickness (this is the standard criticism, also made by Dahnke et al. as quoted above).

**Deep-learning reconstruction**
- CorticalFlow (Lebrat et al., NeurIPS 2021) reports a 17.38% average Chamfer-distance reduction compared with DeepCSR [V-abs] — [NeurIPS 2021 paper](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/f6b5f8c32c65fee991049a55dc97d1ce-Paper.pdf). CorticalFlow++ [V-abs] — [arXiv 2206.06598](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.06598).
- TopoFit (Hoopes, Iglesias, Fischl, Greve, Dalca; MIDL 2022, PMLR v172): "1.8x more accurate than the current state-of-the-art deep-learning strategy", and robust to WM hypointensities [V-abs] — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v172/hoopes22a.html).
- [U] DeepCSR (Cruz et al., CVPR 2021), PialNN (Ma et al., IPMI 2021), FastSurfer (Henschel et al., NeuroImage 2020), and recon-all-clinical (Gopinath et al., 2024) are all trained on FreeSurfer outputs (or synthetic data derived from FreeSurfer). They therefore inherit FreeSurfer's collision-style midline placement in buried sulci as their "ground truth". Their thickness prior is implicit and population-level, learned from ~1 mm FreeSurfer labels.
- Ravikumar S, …, Yushkevich PA et al. "Improved Segmentation of Deep Sulci in Cortical Gray Matter Using a Deep Learning Framework Incorporating Laplace's Equation." IPMI 2023 [V-abs]. Uses ex vivo human MTL MRI and a Laplace-based loss that "locally penalize[s] unresolved boundaries between tightly folded sulci" — [arXiv 2303.00795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00795).

### Inferences
- The user's finding (equal arrival puts the contact at the midpoint and fails for M1 vs S1) applies to FreeSurfer collision, DELFMAP collisions, ACE and PBT distance-maxima skeletons, and DiReCT's "symmetric banks" whenever no CSF is visible. Each of them places the contact at a point that is equidistant in some metric (Euclidean WM distance, a potential field, or a registration). Any asymmetry comes only from data such as residual CSF intensity, never from a thickness expectation.
- PBT's claim to handle "sulcal asymmetries" is the one statement that might contradict this. It must be checked in the full text before claiming novelty.

### Gaps
- Exact PBT algorithm for asymmetric blurred sulci (full text not retrieved).
- No FreeSurfer document found that describes pial placement in fully fused sulci explicitly. The FreeSurfer source code (`mrisurf_deform`, `MRIScomputeBorderValues`) would be the authoritative reference.
- `mris_place_surface` options: no doc page retrieved this session.

## (b) Thickness priors, maximum-thickness limits, and propagation from neighbouring cortex

### Takeaway
Hard upper bounds are common: FreeSurfer reports thickness only up to 5 mm, DELFMAP has d_max = 6 mm, and DiReCT has τ, 5 mm in the clinical study and 10 mm by default in KellyKapowski. Only DiReCT accepts a spatially varying prior image, and even that is a cap on displacement, not an expected value. PBT propagates or projects a thickness value along each cortical column, but within the column, not laterally from visible-pial neighbours. I found no tool that carries a thickness estimate laterally from cortex where the pial surface is visible into a fused region and uses it to place the contact.

### Cited Findings
- FreeSurfer: the thickness computation thresholds at a maximum "-max <max> … (default=5mm)". A user thread reports that longitudinal runs seemed to use 3.5 mm, and a maintainer replied that thickness "is not constrained to 3.5mm" (the full reply was not retrieved) [V-abs] — [FreeSurfer mailing list](https://mail.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/archives/list/freesurfer@nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/thread/SMW6JU25IGDV2CAOZR5MG3YBTLIUVK4O/). A third-party reimplementation also lists `max_thickness = 5` [V-abs, weaker evidence] — [ravetools docs](https://rdrr.io/cran/ravetools/man/mris_make_surfaces.html). [U] As I understand it, this 5 mm value is a reporting cap (vertices above it are set to 0 or excluded), and it also limits how far the pial search goes from the white surface in surface placement.
- DiReCT [V]: thickness is "guaranteed to stay within a range that is set by user-defined, spatially varying prior constraints" τ(x). A point's Euclidean displacement must stay below τ(x) at every time step, and points at the limit get zero update. The clinical study used τ = 5 mm. In Fig. 8, a very high prior (18) effectively removes the constraint, while a spatially varying prior (6 on one bank) gives asymmetric bank thickness — [PMC2836782](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836782). **This is the closest existing precedent to the user's per-bank expected thickness, but it works as a cap.**
- DiReCT's volumetric propagation [V]: thickness is accumulated along the deformation path and divided by the hit count (T_vol = I_total / I_hit), so it is spread through the GM column — [PMC2836782](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836782).
- KellyKapowski options [V]: `-t/--thickness-prior-estimate` "prior constraint on the final thickness measurement", default 10 mm; `-a/--thickness-prior-image` spatially varying prior (no default); `-l` smoothing variance of the hit/total images (default 1.0); `-m` velocity-field smoothing (1.5, or B-spline mesh spacing 15); `-r` gradient step 0.025; `-c` convergence [50, 0.001, 10] — [KellyKapowski man page](https://dyn.manpages.debian.org/unstable/ants/KellyKapowski.1.en.html).
- DELFMAP [V]: a distance-constraining factor with d_max = 6 mm ("above the anatomical upper bound on cortical thickness"). The skeleton uses d_min ≈ 1 mm (the lower bound on thickness). The stopping factor is a sigmoid with K = 40 and P0 = 0.8, and P0 and d_max are the most sensitive parameters — [PMC3296314](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/).
- PBT [V-abs]: projects local maxima of the WM distance onto GM voxels, i.e., propagation along the column — [abstract snippet](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/fumie.costen/pastwork/DATA/readingmaterial/corticalthickness4.pdf). [U] I recall an additional correction step in CAT12 for overestimated thickness in blurred regions, but did not verify it.
- [U] ASP/CLASP (MacDonald 2000; Kim 2005) and Zeng 1999 use global min/max distance bands between the inner and outer surfaces.

### Inferences
- How to frame novelty: existing priors are (i) global caps (FreeSurfer, DELFMAP, Zeng, ASP) or (ii) a spatially varying cap that the user supplies (DiReCT `-a`). A thickness field that is *estimated* from visible-pial neighbours, *propagated laterally* with a smoothness energy, and *used as an expectation* that sets relative front speed or contact position appears absent from the main tools surveyed.
- A practical baseline to compare against: run KellyKapowski with `--thickness-prior-image` set to the user's propagated thickness field. This tests whether a cap alone reproduces the contact-placement gain.

### Gaps
- No verified numeric range for the ASP, CLASP, or Zeng thickness bands.
- Whether FreeSurfer's 5 mm limit also limits the pial search distance during placement (not only the reported thickness) was not confirmed from source.

## (c) Probabilistic or uncertainty treatment

### Takeaway
None of the surveyed surface or thickness tools reports a posterior over contact or pial position, or a per-vertex thickness uncertainty, in fused sulci. Their "probabilistic" parts are limited to fuzzy or probabilistic tissue memberships used as forces or permittivities (CRUISE fuzzy classification, DELFMAP GM-probability permittivity, CAT12/SPM tissue probabilities in PBT) and multi-atlas label fusion as extra evidence (MaCRUISE).

### Cited Findings
- CRUISE uses fuzzy tissue classification as input to the deformable model [V-abs] — [USUHS record](https://scholar.usuhs.edu/en/publications/cruise-cortical-reconstruction-using-implicit-surface-evolution/).
- DELFMAP sets the dielectric permittivity from the GM probability [V] — [PMC3296314](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/).
- MaCRUISE adds multi-atlas segmentation evidence where CSF is not visible [V-abs] — [PMC4927397](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4927397).
- PBT uses partial-volume tissue segmentation [V-abs] — [abstract snippet](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/fumie.costen/pastwork/DATA/readingmaterial/corticalthickness4.pdf).

### Inferences
- A Bayesian contact posterior (likelihood from intensity at the contact, prior from the propagated thickness field with its own uncertainty) would be new relative to these tools. It would also give the uncertainty maps that none of them provides.

### Gaps
- I did not find (in this limited search) Bayesian or probabilistic cortical-surface placement papers with per-vertex uncertainty. A targeted search is still needed, for example: "Bayesian cortical thickness uncertainty", "probabilistic surface reconstruction cortex uncertainty quantification", Monte Carlo dropout for DL surfaces, or "probabilistic thickness" in ex vivo hippocampal work by the Yushkevich group. Treat this as not established either way.

## (d) Known failure modes in buried sulci and how they were validated

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are thickness overestimation in blurred or fused regions when no sulcal model is used, symmetric (midline) bank splits when CSF is invisible, spurious or discontinuous skeletons (ACE), and methods tuned for 1 mm data that do not scale to 0.25–0.35 mm. Validation is mostly phantoms with known thickness, test–retest, and inter-method agreement. None of the papers surveyed validates contact position against histology in fused sulci with banks of unequal thickness.

### Cited Findings
- Without sulcal modelling, methods "tend to overestimate thickness in blurred regions" [V-abs] — Dahnke et al. 2013, via [snippet](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/fumie.costen/pastwork/DATA/readingmaterial/corticalthickness4.pdf).
- DiReCT: with no CSF evidence the banks come out symmetric, and fundus thickness is overestimated where there is no depth information. Validation used 3D phantoms (sphere, ribbon, ribbon with sulcus; r_w = 7 mm, r_g = 10 mm, true thickness 3 mm). At 1.0 mm voxels the estimates were ~2.75–3.25 mm, approaching 3 mm at finer resolution, at none/low/high noise. A clinical study had 20 FTD patients at ~12.4-month scan intervals. The authors state there was no quantitative ground-truth comparison on real data and no FreeSurfer comparison — [PMC2836782](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836782).
- DELFMAP validation [V]: simulations with unresolved-fundus and bridged-sulcus cases, where it found a plausible boundary with no minimum-distance cutoff (unlike ACE). On 20 OASIS subjects (40 test–retest sessions): WM precision 0.19 mm (FreeSurfer) vs 0.24 mm (DELFMAP); pial precision ~0.24–0.25 mm for both; inter-method pial distance 0.40/0.42 mm (L/R), with ~7% of vertices > 1 mm; inter-method thickness mean absolute difference 0.35/0.34 mm, with DELFMAP thicker by +0.12/+0.11 mm. Ex vivo: 3 left hemispheres at 0.25–0.35 mm isotropic, mean thickness 2.2 ± 0.7 mm. Run time was 67 min at 0.35 mm and 3 h 20 min with 4.7 GB at 0.25 mm, and manual editing was needed for ventricles and large topology defects — [PMC3296314](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/).
- Failure modes listed there [V]: ACE skeletons with spurious branches and discontinuities, a needed WM-distance cutoff, and noise sensitivity. CRUISE gave inadequate pial results at 0.35 mm with default settings. CLASP is inaccurate where banks are unresolved, and its self-proximity cost is prohibitive at high resolution. FreeSurfer's self-intersection checks are expensive and the pipeline is 1 mm-oriented — [PMC3296314](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/).
- MaACE detects sulci that ACE misses when CSF is not visible [V-abs] — [PMC4927397](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4927397).
- Ex vivo deep sulci [V-abs]: partial-volume effects make opposing banks appear fused, and the resulting "bridged" sulci cause thickness errors. The Ravikumar et al. Laplace loss targets this in MTL ex vivo MRI — [arXiv 2303.00795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00795). A 7T whole-body ex vivo dataset of 37 specimens with deep-learning segmentation (Penn group) is relevant [V-abs, search summary only; exact citation not checked] — [catalyzex author page](https://www.catalyzex.com/author/Edward%20B.%20Lee).
- Deep learning: no sulcus-stratified (by depth or fusion) error analysis was found for DeepCSR, CorticalFlow, or TopoFit. Their reported metrics are global Chamfer/ASSD distances [V-abs] — [CorticalFlow NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/f6b5f8c32c65fee991049a55dc97d1ce-Paper.pdf), [TopoFit](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v172/hoopes22a.html).

### Inferences
- A validation design that would separate the user's method from all of these: fused-sulcus cases with banks of unequal thickness (M1/S1 in the central sulcus) and ground truth from histology or from adjacent sections where the sulcus is open. Report the signed contact offset. Prior work reports only symmetric phantoms (DiReCT's 3 mm ribbon) or inter-method agreement.
- The DiReCT Fig. 8 asymmetric-prior example is a small qualitative demonstration that per-bank priors change the split. It is worth citing as precedent and as a baseline.

### Gaps
- No paper found that measures contact or midline error in fused sulci against histology.
- Ex vivo pipelines at 100–200 µm that explicitly address kissing sulci: only DELFMAP (250–350 µm) and the Penn MTL/7T work were found. A targeted search is still needed: Wang/Fischl ex vivo surface work, Edlow et al. 2019 100 µm brain, BigBrain surfaces (Lewis et al., Wagstyl et al. 2020 PLoS Biol, which uses a laminar-informed approach on 20 µm histology), and the Khandelwal et al. 2024 7T postmortem segmentation paper.
