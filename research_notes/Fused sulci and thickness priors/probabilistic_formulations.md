# Probabilistic and energy formulations for propagating thickness laterally and placing a boundary between two facing layered stacks

Scope: precedents for (a) the proposed energy E(t) = Σ_c w_c (t_c − t̂_c)² + λ Σ_{c~c'} ρ(t_c − t_c') on the WM-surface column graph, with mean and uncertainty, and (b) the contact surface between two facing banks as a posterior: x ~ N(t_A, σ_A²), L − x ~ N(t_B, σ_B²), plus a dark-line likelihood and lateral smoothness.

Verification legend: **[V]** means the title, authors, venue, year and DOI/URL were checked against a primary or index source in this session. **[V-meta]** means only the metadata or abstract was checked, not the equations. **[U]** means unverified: the item comes from background knowledge and was not checked in this session.

## 1. Soft thickness and shape priors in layered (column-graph) segmentation, and how OCT methods represent thickness uncertainty

### Takeaway
The Iowa graph-search line (Garvin 2009, then Song 2013) is the closest discrete analogue to the proposed energy. Song et al. replace hard smoothness and thickness bounds with soft convex pairwise penalties on surface-position differences ("shape prior") and on inter-surface distance changes ("context prior"), and they still get a global optimum by max-flow. These methods give a MAP surface only, with no uncertainty. Rathke et al. 2014 is the main precedent for a full posterior over layer boundaries. It uses a Gaussian shape prior combined with a discrete appearance model, solved by variational inference that alternates a Gaussian factor with column chains. Its uncertainty is reported as entropy and mutual information, not as thickness credible intervals. Deep-learning OCT methods give per-pixel MC-dropout uncertainty maps.

### Cited Findings
- **Song Q, Bai J, Garvin MK, Sonka M, Buatti JM, Wu X. "Optimal multiple surface segmentation with shape and context priors." IEEE TMI 32(2):376–386, 2013. DOI 10.1109/TMI.2012.2227120, PMCID PMC4076846, PMID 23193309. [V-meta]** — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:23193309&resultType=core&format=json); [Iowa repository](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Optimal-Multiple-Surface-Segmentation-With-Shape/9984040396902771)
  - The method uses an arc-based graph representation. Priors enter as pairwise energy terms: a shape-prior term penalizes local shape changes, and a context-prior term penalizes local changes in surface distance. The global optimum comes from one max-flow in low-order polynomial time. — [Europe PMC abstract](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:23193309&resultType=core&format=json)
  - On intraretinal OCT, the mean unsigned surface error fell from 6.30 ± 1.58 µm (conventional graph search) to 5.14 ± 0.99 µm. — [same](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:23193309&resultType=core&format=json)
  - Functional form, from background knowledge **[U, check against the paper]**: E = Σ_i Σ_p D_i(p, s_i(p)) + Σ_i Σ_{(p,q)} f_{i}(s_i(p) − s_i(q) − m_{i,pq}) + Σ_{(i,j)} Σ_p g_{ij}(s_i(p) − s_j(p) − d̄_{ij}(p)). Here m and d̄ are the expected neighbour offset and the expected inter-surface distance, learned from training surfaces. f and g must be convex, which is the condition for the arc-weighted graph to give an exact max-flow optimum. A quadratic f has the same form as the proposed λ(t_c − t_c')² term, and g has the same form as the w_c(t_c − t̂_c)² term. Non-convex ρ (truncated or Tukey) breaks exactness. Huber and L1 are convex and stay exact.

- **Rathke F, Schmidt S, Schnörr C. "Probabilistic intra-retinal layer segmentation in 3-D OCT images using global shape regularization." Medical Image Analysis 18:781–794, 2014. The shape prior is p(b) = N(b; µ, Σ), with Σ regularized by probabilistic PCA (Σ = WWᵀ + σ²I, q = 20 components). The q_b step is closed-form: Σ̄ = (K + P̃)⁻¹, and the mean comes from conjugate gradient. Here P̃ is the precision contributed by the expected data/shape terms, which is the same "prior precision + data precision" structure as the proposed GMRF. Run time is about 1 minute per 3-D volume. — [arXiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1403.8003)
  - Reported uncertainty: a global quality index built from the mutual information between neighbouring boundary variables plus the shape term, which correlates with unsigned error at r = 0.82. Column-wise MI sorts regions into three confidence classes. The shape term separates glaucoma from healthy scans (AUC 0.84 for pre-perimetric scans). The paper does **not** report thickness credible intervals. — [arXiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1403.8003)
  - Errors: 2.46 µm on 35 3-D volumes; 2.92 µm (normal) and 4.09 µm (glaucoma) on 2-D circular scans. — [Heidelberg page](https://hci.iwr.uni-heidelberg.de/node/6791)
- **Deep Bayesian OCT layer segmentation** (MC dropout / Bayesian U-Net):
  - Sedai et al. 2018, "Joint segmentation and uncertainty visualization of retinal layers in OCT images using Bayesian deep learning", arXiv 1809.04282, which reports per-pixel uncertainty maps that point to segmentation errors. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.04282) [V-meta]
  - Orlando et al. 2019, U2-Net, arXiv 1901.07929, where epistemic uncertainty is inversely correlated with performance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.07929) [V-meta]

### Inferences
- The proposed E(t) is a 1-surface case of Song's model, with thickness as the variable and t̂_c as the context-prior expectation. It is also the MAP of Rathke's Gaussian part with a sparse (Laplacian) precision in place of a dense PPCA precision. When ρ is quadratic, the mean is the solution of the sparse system (W + λL) t = W t̂, and the posterior covariance is (W + λL)⁻¹/σ² (see §2). The Song-style max-flow is not needed in that case.
- Rathke's mean-field split maps directly onto the contact problem: a Gaussian continuous factor (thickness, contact position) plus a discrete per-column factor (dark-line voxel position), alternated.
- The OCT literature reports uncertainty as quality flags (MI, entropy, pixel variance), not as calibrated thickness intervals. Calibration is therefore an open validation task for this project, not something it can borrow.

## 2. Gaussian MRFs / Gaussian processes on surfaces and meshes for interpolating sparse measurements with uncertainty; robust and edge-preserving variants

### Takeaway
The quadratic version of the proposed energy is a GMRF. The standard rigorous form is the SPDE/FEM construction of Lindgren, Rue and Lindström (2011), which gives a Matérn field on any triangulated manifold with a sparse precision built from mesh mass and stiffness matrices. Graph Matérn GPs (Borovitskiy et al. 2021) are the graph-Laplacian analogue. Heat-kernel smoothing (Chung 2005) is the cortical-imaging precedent for smoothing along the surface, but it smooths only and gives no posterior. Edge-preserving priors (TV, Laplace increments, Cauchy differences) keep kinks at borders but lose closed-form uncertainty. TV priors also have a known discretization pathology: they are not discretization-invariant. Cauchy/heavy-tailed difference priors fix this but need MCMC.

### Cited Findings
- **Lindgren F, Rue H, Lindström J. "An explicit link between Gaussian fields and Gaussian Markov random fields: the stochastic partial differential equation approach." JRSS-B 73(4):423–498, 2011 (with discussion). DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9868.2011.00777.x [DOI: U; venue and pages V].** The paper builds a finite-element representation of a Matérn Gaussian field on an unstructured triangulation using piecewise-linear bases. The FEM weights form a GMRF with a sparse precision, and the paper gives explicit links between the SPDE parameters and the precision entries. — [Bath research portal](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/an-explicit-link-between-gaussian-fields-and-gaussian-markov-rand/); [PDF](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/peter/591/Lindgren.pdf)
  - The SPDE family has since been extended to non-stationary and non-Gaussian fields on arbitrary manifolds (Lindgren, Bolin, Rue 2021 review). — [arXiv 2111.01084](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.01084) [V-meta]
  - Standard form, from background knowledge [U]: for α = 2, (κ² − Δ)u = W gives Q = τ²(κ⁴C + 2κ²G + G C⁻¹ G), with C the (lumped) mass matrix and G the stiffness (cotangent Laplacian). A spatially varying κ(s) gives a non-stationary field. For example, a lower κ (longer range) inside an area and a higher κ near areal borders.
- **Borovitskiy V, Azangulov I, Terenin A, Mostowsky P, Deisenroth M, Durrande N. "Matérn Gaussian processes on graphs." AISTATS 2021, PMLR 130:2593–2601. [V]** The paper extends the SPDE view of Matérn GPs to weighted undirected graphs: K = (2ν/κ² I + L)^(−ν), computed with Laplacian eigenpairs (graph Fourier features). It is compatible with inducing points and non-conjugate likelihoods. — [arXiv 2010.15538](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.15538); [ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/aistats/2021/borovitskiy2021aistats-matern)
- **Chung MK, Robbins SM, Dalton KM, Davidson RJ, Alexander AL, Evans AC. "Cortical thickness analysis in autism with heat kernel smoothing." NeuroImage 25:1256–1265, 2005. [V-meta]** The paper argues that thickness on a folded cortex should be smoothed with weights based on geodesic distance (the heat kernel) rather than 3-D Euclidean distance. — [PDF](https://pages.stat.wisc.edu/~mchung/papers/ni_heatkernel.pdf)
- **GP priors on cortical surfaces for Bayesian regression:** a 2023 arXiv paper (2306.03663) on group-level cortical image-on-scalar regression uses GP priors with great-circle distance on the sphere over about 30k template vertices. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03663) [V-meta; authors not checked]
- **Albrecht T, Lüthi M, Gerig T, Vetter T. "Posterior shape models." Medical Image Analysis 17(8):959–973, 2013. [V-meta]** The paper conditions a statistical shape model on partial observations, and the result is again a Gaussian shape model. It shows that common approaches are equivalent to PPCA and GP regression. Lüthi et al.'s later Gaussian Process Morphable Models generalize this to arbitrary GP kernels, using a low-rank Karhunen–Loève/Nyström representation. — [Basel repository](https://sonar.ch/global/documents/17953); [GPMM](https://deepai.org/publication/gaussian-process-morphable-models)
- **Edge-preserving priors and their uncertainty:**
  - TV priors are the usual edge-preserving choice, but Lassas & Siltanen (2004) showed that the TV prior is not discretization-invariant: as the mesh is refined, the posterior converges to a Gaussian-like smooth or degenerate limit. — [Markkanen et al., arXiv 1603.06135](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.06135)
  - Markkanen M, Roininen L, Huttunen JMJ, Lasanen S. "Cauchy difference priors for edge-preserving Bayesian inversion." J. Inverse Ill-Posed Problems 27(2), 2019. DOI 10.1515/jiip-2017-0048. Cauchy random-walk increments stay non-Gaussian in the discretization limit and keep jumps. Posterior sampling uses single-component Metropolis–Hastings. — [arXiv 1603.06135](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.06135); [De Gruyter](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jiip-2017-0048/pdf) [V-meta; author list U]
  - Bardsley's Laplace-increment prior gives reconstructions qualitatively similar to TV. — [UMT record](https://umimpact.umt.edu/en/publications/laplace-distributed-increments-the-laplace-prior-and-edge-preserv/) [V-meta]
  - Cauchy MRF priors on general graphs: arXiv 2105.12488. They are motivated by the failure of Gaussian priors to recover edges. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.12488) [V-meta]
  - Besov priors (Lassas–Saksman–Siltanen; Dashti–Harris–Stuart) are the well-posed infinite-dimensional alternative to TV. — [Markkanen et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.06135)

### Inferences
- **Recommended quadratic core (exact uncertainty):** take ρ(u) = u²/2, with W = diag(w_c) and graph Laplacian L. Then the posterior is t | data ~ N(µ, Σ), with precision Q = W + λL, mean Qµ = W t̂, and Σ = Q⁻¹ (all scaled by the noise variance if w_c = 1/σ_c²). The marginal variances diag(Q⁻¹) come from sparse Cholesky plus Takahashi selected inversion, or approximately from Hutchinson / Rao–Blackwellized sampling: draw z ~ N(0, Q⁻¹) by solving Lᵀz = ε with the Cholesky factor. This is the standard GMRF toolkit, as in R-INLA [U for the specific selected-inversion citation]. Columns with w_c = 0 (fused areas) get variance from the prior alone, and that variance grows with graph distance from evidence. This is the qualitatively correct behaviour.
- **Pitfall 1, improper prior:** L alone is rank-deficient, because the constant mode is unpenalized. Any connected component of the column graph with no evidence (Σ w_c = 0) has infinite variance. Add a weak global prior on thickness, κ²I (the SPDE/Matérn form), so that variance saturates at a population SD rather than diverging.
- **Pitfall 2, mesh dependence:** a plain graph-Laplacian penalty λΣ(t_c − t_c')² depends on edge density and lengths. Use cotangent/FEM weights with a mass matrix (the SPDE form), so that λ means the same physical correlation length (mm) everywhere and under remeshing. At 160 µm this matters near fundi, where column density changes.
- **Pitfall 3, first vs second order:** a first-order (membrane) prior gives a Brownian-like interpolant whose variance grows linearly with distance from data and which creates "tent" artefacts between evidence points. A second-order (thin-plate, α = 2, Q ∝ G C⁻¹ G) prior extrapolates trends, which suits thickness gradients across a bank. The user's "second-order smoothness allowing kinks" corresponds to α = 2 with a robust penalty on the second difference.
- **Pitfall 4, robust ρ and uncertainty:** with Huber/TV/Cauchy ρ there is no closed-form posterior. Options:
  - (a) A Laplace approximation at the MAP, H = W + λ Σ ρ''(Δ) DᵀD, where D is the edge-difference operator. ρ'' is zero in the linear (outlier) regime of Huber and negative for Cauchy/Tukey, so H can become singular or indefinite across a detected kink. The Laplace approximation then *decouples* the two sides of the kink, which is the desired behaviour but can understate uncertainty about whether the kink exists.
  - (b) An IRLS / half-quadratic "line process" view. A robust ρ equals a Gaussian with per-edge latent weights (Geman–Reynolds / Black–Rangarajan duality [U]). Conditional on the edge weights the field is a GMRF with exact covariance, so the Laplace or variational-Bayes covariance is (W + λDᵀΩD)⁻¹ with Ω the converged IRLS weights. This is cheap and interpretable, because it says which edges were "cut".
  - (c) Gibbs sampling over latent scale mixtures (a Student-t or Cauchy difference prior as a Gaussian scale mixture) for honest uncertainty, at greater cost.
  - (d) If areal borders are known a priori (e.g. from atlas or landmark registration), down-weight those edges deterministically and keep the problem fully Gaussian. This is simplest and keeps exact uncertainty.
- **Pitfall 5, TV discretization:** TV-type priors behave differently as resolution changes (Lassas–Siltanen). Since the column graph comes from a 160 µm mesh that may be refined, prefer Gaussian+line-process or Cauchy/Student-t increments if posterior uncertainty is to be reported.

### Gaps
- I found no published GP/GMRF on the *cortical mesh* that interpolates *thickness* with posterior variance in an individual subject. The precedents are group-level GP regression and heat-kernel smoothing. This appears to be a novel application of standard machinery.
- I did not verify a specific paper on Laplace approximations for Huber/TV priors in imaging.

## 3. Joint estimation of two coupled surfaces with a shared boundary; posterior fusion with inverse-variance weighting; cortical precedents

### Takeaway
Coupled-surface cortical segmentation is well established (Zeng et al. 1999: two level sets coupled by a thickness-dependent speed; graph-search multi-surface with inter-surface distance constraints). These methods are deterministic: thickness enters as a hard band or a speed penalty. I found no probabilistic precedent that places the contact between two *facing* banks as a posterior. The fusion step itself is textbook Gaussian conditioning (a product of Gaussians), so the novelty lies in the combination and in handling non-Gaussian dark-line evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **Zeng X, Staib LH, Schultz RT, Duncan JS. "Segmentation and measurement of the cortex from 3-D MR images using coupled-surfaces propagation." IEEE TMI 18(10):927–937, 1999. [V-meta via DBLP listing]** Two embedded level-set surfaces evolve simultaneously, each driven by its own image term and coupled by a near-constant-thickness constraint. Secondary sources describe the coupling as a propagation speed that decreases when the surfaces get too close or too far apart, and as keeping them about 3 mm apart. — [DBLP](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/pid/36/5199.html); [CVPR 1998 precursor](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/1998/zeng1998cvpr-volumetric)
  - Later work notes that coupled level sets cannot represent two boundaries closer than a voxel. This is directly relevant to fused banks, where the two pial surfaces coincide. — [Goldenberg/Kimmel et al.](https://csaws.cs.technion.ac.il/~ehudr/publications/pdf/GoldenbergKRR02a.pdf) [V-meta]
- **Song et al. 2013 context prior** (see §1): a soft penalty on the deviation of the inter-surface distance from its expected value. It is the discrete, MAP-only analogue of "L − x ~ N(t_B, σ_B²)". — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:23193309&resultType=core&format=json)
- **Rathke 2014** gives a joint Gaussian prior over *all* boundaries, including inter-boundary covariance, with a posterior from VB. This is the closest probabilistic precedent for joint multi-surface placement, although its surfaces are stacked, not facing. — [arXiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1403.8003)

### Inferences (derivations; standard Gaussian algebra, no external source needed)
- **Per-column contact posterior (Gaussian part):** let L be the WM-to-WM distance along the column pair (or along a shared path). Then x ~ N(t_A, σ_A²) and L − x ~ N(t_B, σ_B²). The second term is a likelihood on x with mean L − t_B and variance σ_B². The product gives
  - σ_x² = (1/σ_A² + 1/σ_B²)⁻¹,  x̂ = σ_x² (t_A/σ_A² + (L − t_B)/σ_B²).
  - The implied inconsistency r = L − t_A − t_B has predictive distribution N(0, σ_A² + σ_B² + σ_L²). A large |r|/√(…) is a model-check statistic. It flags a wrong L (a path that does not cross the fused zone), a missing CSF gap, or a wrong areal prior. Report it per column.
  - Pitfall: the uncertainty in L (from WM surface localization) is usually ignored. Including σ_L² inflates σ_x² only through the B term: σ_B² becomes σ_B² + σ_L².
- **Adding the dark line:** the dark-line likelihood ℓ(x) is typically multimodal (several candidate minima) or flat. The posterior is then p(x) ∝ N(x; x̂, σ_x²) · ℓ(x). Evaluate it on the discrete samples of the column (1-D, so cheap and exact), and report the mean/MAP plus the posterior SD or a 95 % HPD. A Gaussian approximation is wrong when ℓ is bimodal. Keep the discrete 1-D posterior per column, as in Rathke's q_c chain factor.
- **Adding lateral smoothness:** coupling the x_c across columns turns the problem into a chain/grid MRF over discrete x_c. Options:
  - (i) Song/LOGISMOS-style max-flow with convex pairwise f(x_c − x_c') gives an exact MAP, and Kohli–Torr min-marginals give per-column uncertainty (§4).
  - (ii) Rathke-style VB: a Gaussian field over continuous x plus per-column discrete dark-line factors, alternated.
  - (iii) If ℓ is approximated as Gaussian where it is unimodal (with inflated variance elsewhere), everything is a GMRF solved by the same sparse (W + λL) machinery as §2, now with a combined data precision 1/σ_A² + 1/σ_B² + 1/σ_ℓ².
- **Double counting:** t_A and t_B come from the §2 propagation and are spatially correlated (the off-diagonal entries of Σ_A). Treating per-column marginals as independent and then adding a fresh lateral smoothness prior on x double-counts smoothness and makes σ_x too small. Cleaner: solve one joint GMRF over (t_A, t_B, x) with the constraint x + y = L built in, or use the full joint precision. The joint precision stays sparse, because the coupling is only the column pairing between banks.
- **Asymmetric evidence:** if bank A has a visible pial surface (small σ_A) and B has none, x̂ follows A. That is correct, but it means B's thickness at the contact is then *inferred* as L − x̂. Report it as derived, not measured, or circularity enters downstream thickness statistics.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that models the boundary between two *facing* cortical banks (fused sulcus / fused gyral walls) probabilistically. I searched coupled surfaces and cortical uncertainty; I did not search ex-vivo fused-sulcus papers specifically, since that is outside this note's scope.
- I did not retrieve the exact Zeng 1999 speed-coupling function.

## 4. Uncertainty quantification for cortical surface placement and thickness; uncertainty for graph-cut / max-flow solutions

### Takeaway
Uncertainty for cortical surface placement is a recent and thin literature. UNSURF (Mehta, Gopinath, Glocker, Iglesias 2025) reports that voxel-wise MC-dropout/ensemble variance correlates weakly or even negatively with thickness error. A discrepancy between the predicted SDF and the SDF of the fitted surface works much better. For discrete graph-search solutions, min-marginal energies (Kohli & Torr) give exact per-node uncertainty at the cost of dynamic graph-cut re-solves. Tarlow & Adams show how to calibrate them.

### Cited Findings
- **Mehta R, Gopinath K, Glocker B, Iglesias JE. "UNSURF: Uncertainty quantification for cortical surface reconstruction of clinical brain MRIs." arXiv 2506.00498 (v2); MICCAI 2025 (paper 0304). [V]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00498v2); [MICCAI 2025 page](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2025/0989-Paper0304.html)
  - Uncertainty is defined per voxel as U = (D̂ − D̃)², where D̂ is the CNN-predicted SDF and D̃ is the SDF of the final fitted (topology-corrected) surface. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00498v2)
  - Ensemble-dropout variance (5 models × 20 passes) showed weak or negative correlation with thickness error. The authors argue that variance reflects only prediction dispersion and ignores the geometric/topological processing. Propagating samples through mesh fitting would cost about 2 h per sample. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00498v2)
  - Filtering parcels and nodes by U reduced thickness errors and increased AD-vs-CN effect sizes in ADNI. Sensitivity was lower for thinner slices (2–3 mm). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00498v2)
  - The authors state that uncertainty quantification is needed to identify unreliable measurements in downstream cortical thickness group studies. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00498)
- **Kohli P, Torr PHS. "Measuring uncertainty in graph cut solutions." Computer Vision and Image Understanding 112(1):30–38, 2008. ECCV 2006 version: "Efficiently computing min-marginal energies using dynamic graph cuts". [V-meta]** Exact min-marginals for each node and label via dynamic graph cuts, unlike loopy BP. — [ORA Oxford](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:26452a16-6478-4191-a1db-3aeed392488e); [ECCV 2006](https://mlanthology.org/eccv/2006/kohli2006eccv-measuring)
  - Tarlow D, Adams RP. "Revisiting uncertainty in graph cut solutions." CVPR 2012. The paper interprets min-marginals as exact marginals of a specific (perturbation-like) model, and calibrated min-marginal uncertainties beat baselines after maximum-likelihood training. — [ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2012/tarlow2012cvpr-revisiting) [V-meta]
- **Miller MI et al. "Bayesian construction of geometrically based cortical thickness metrics." NeuroImage 2000** appears only as a citation in a Chung HBM abstract. Content, authors and DOI were not retrieved. **[U]** — [HBM 2004 abstract](https://pages.stat.wisc.edu/~mchung/papers/HBM2004/HBM2004smooth.htm)

### Inferences
- UNSURF's lesson carries over: uncertainty must be computed *on the quantity actually reported* (the final surface or thickness after all constraints), not on an upstream voxel classifier. In this project that argues for taking uncertainty from the posterior of the thickness/contact model itself (§2–3), not from the noise of the dark-line detector alone.
- If the project keeps the existing max-flow column-graph machinery for the contact surface, min-marginals give a "how much does the energy rise if the contact moves by k voxels in column c" curve. exp(−ΔE/T) is then a pseudo-posterior whose temperature T must be calibrated (Tarlow & Adams), for example against held-out columns where the pial surface is visible.
- **Validation suggestion** (inference): mask visible-pial columns as "fused", rerun propagation, and check z-scores (t_true − µ_c)/σ_c. Their SD should be about 1, and coverage of 95 % intervals should be about 95 %, stratified by graph distance to the nearest evidence and by whether an areal border lies between. This is the leave-out calibration the OCT literature does not provide.
