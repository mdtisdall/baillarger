# Build order in; let contrast decide placement

**Bottom line.** The laminar and layered-surface literature points to one rule. **A constraint that holds everywhere (ordering, non-crossing, zero thickness allowed, sub-voxel boundaries, topology, closed regions) belongs in the representation. A constraint that holds only on average (lateral smoothness, thickness continuity, equivolume depth, band templates, contact placement) belongs in the energy.** Every method that broke this rule needed a patch, and each patch repaired a failure that the representation had let through:

- **BigBrain 2020** classifies each column on its own. It then adds column-vector smoothing to stop self-intersections, interpolation for failed columns, and surface smoothing for jitter.
- **Retinal shortest-path methods** get ordering from search windows. They then need a fovea detector because those windows exclude merged layers.
- **Unsupervised MRI clustering** has no ordering or spatial prior. It then discards "noise clusters" at the tissue boundaries and maps clusters to layers by hand.
- **Voxel-grid level sets** force every layer to be at least one voxel thick. The same floor is behind the current code's one-voxel fluid seam.

The planned design (monotone z_c(T), dz/dT ≥ 0, sub-voxel boundaries, Potts regions, a joint pial surface, a zero-thickness seam) already places most constraints correctly. The literature adds four things the plan does not yet have:

1. **R2* depends on cortical orientation to B0.** This can create false facies changes.
2. **"Present but invisible" must be a separate output from "pinched out".** No surveyed method models this up front.
3. **Explicit partial-volume or boundary classes at the pia and the white matter.**
4. **A contrast-defined anchor for T = 0.**

The main conflict with the plan is resolution. The stria of Gennari is about 250–300 µm thick, **about 1.5–2 voxels at 160 µm**, which is below the 2–3 voxel minimum for a target band. A second conflict concerns texture. M1 is "astriate" only because its Baillarger bands are not distinct; its laminar contrast comes from radial fibre bundles. Radial and tangential fibres look the same to the structure tensor at 160 µm (planar, by the depth variation of fibre density), so the orientation term needs no special case there (a cross-echo structure tensor on M1S1 and V1V2 interior cortex gave median λ2/λ1 ≤ 0.20 everywhere, i.e. planar; no linear radial-texture signature at 160 µm). The findings add cases to T0, T1, T4, T5 and T6, and add a pinch-out validation metric. A B0-orientation test (T8) is kept only as a future check.

Notation. **[V]** means the research notes confirmed the citation against a primary record or index. **[U]** means unverified or read only in part. "Established" paragraphs report sources. "Inference" paragraphs are reasoning from them.

## Every surveyed method patched what its representation left out

**Established.** Wagstyl et al. 2020 built the BigBrain six-layer atlas from a 1D CNN that labels each profile independently. It reaches 83 ± 2% per-point accuracy, and layer IV is the least confident class ([Wagstyl 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3000678) [V]). The model has no order, no topology and no lateral coupling, so each of these arrives as a fix-up:

- Surfaces are read from argmax transitions.
- Columns where "the network failed (less than 1%)" are interpolated from neighbours. The paper does not define failure.
- Surface indices get 0.5 mm FWHM smoothing plus 20 iterations of mesh smoothing.
- The pial→white vectors are smoothed "to avoid crossing profiles, which can result in mesh self-intersections". The paper gives this smoothing as 3 mm FWHM in one place and about 2 mm in another, and does not reconcile them.
- No layer-order check and no inter-surface intersection check are described.

The 2018 predecessor placed layer IV by a loop: snap to the nearest intensity peak, apply Taubin smoothing, smooth equivolumetric depth at 10 mm FWHM, and repeat. It reached a **72 µm unsigned error**. That error was measured only after the agranular and dysgranular samples were excluded, because L4 has no peak there ([Wagstyl 2018](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/28/7/2551/4990976) [V]). The notes flag an arithmetic inconsistency in that paper's sample counts (42 − 5 − 1 ≠ 37).

The MRI methods show the same pattern. Bastiani et al. 2016 ran k-means on diffusion profiles at 340 µm with no spatial or ordering prior. They got "noise clusters" at the tissue boundaries, which they discarded. They chose k by silhouette, mapped clusters to layers by hand, and placed areal borders by thresholding Hotelling's T² at 4.5 SD. Layer I (100–200 µm) was sometimes missing. Agreement with histology was 0.92–0.93 for 2–4 layer complexes ([Bastiani 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5102896/) [V]). Fischl et al. 2009 drew entorhinal labels by hand at 100 µm. When they projected the labels onto the surface, they had to repair holes with binary closing (3 dilations, 3 erosions). The borders became hard or impossible to see when the data were coarsened from 100 to 120 µm ([Fischl 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2738987/) [V]). Costantini et al. 2023 had 150 µm multi-echo FLASH, but segmented only the boundary between supragranular and infragranular layers. Their 10 intermediate surfaces were placed by geometry alone ([Costantini 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10569704/) [V]). **No MRI study in the notes fits laminar surfaces to band contrast with ordering and smoothness built in, and none reports per-layer surface error in µm against histology.**

The retinal OCT field shows the contrast between patching and building in most clearly, because it has solved the same problem both ways:

- **Chiu et al. 2010** (patched). Ordering comes from sequential search windows. At the fovea the windows exclude the merged layers. So a detector flags foveal columns (mean inner-layer thickness < 5 px), and the windows are then extended "to handle layer merging" ([Chiu 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3408910) [V]).
- **Li et al. 2006** (built in). One min-cut solves all surfaces jointly. Ordering arcs and the separation bound δl ≥ 0 make coincident surfaces representable ([Li 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2646122) [V]).
- **Garvin et al. 2009** (built in). Per-column thickness bounds are learned, with the minimum "truncated so as not to go below 0" ([Garvin 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2911837) [V]).
- **He et al. 2018** (built in). The network regresses non-negative thicknesses with a ReLU, so the boundaries come out in order ([He 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.05120) [V-arXiv; venue U]).

In both He papers, a second structured stage repairs an unstructured first stage ([He 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7855873) [V]).

**Inference.** The patches cluster into five types: ordering by sequential windows, special-case detectors at merge points, smoothing after the fit, discarding boundary partial-volume classes, and morphological clean-up of labels. Each one has an up-front counterpart in the table below. The planned design already has a counterpart for four of the five types. It has none yet for boundary partial volume.

## Three constraints the plan does not yet carry

**Orientation to B0 (established physics, inferred consequence).** In fixed cortex, R2* dominates GRE contrast. Fischl 2009 measured T2* of 26 ms in GM and 11 ms in WM ([Fischl](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2738987/)). The laminar R2* contrast is mostly iron that follows myelin. Reductive iron extraction cut the R2* difference between the stria and the rest of the cortex from **13.1 to 2.7 s⁻¹** at 7T, 150 µm ([Fukunaga 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2840419/)). Iron plus myelin explained **91% of cortical R2* variance, myelin alone 66%** ([Kirilina 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541072/)). Cortical R2* depends on orientation to B0. In vivo the effect was **strongest in M1 (ΔR2* = 4.10 s⁻¹)** ([Cohen-Adad 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.01.053)). Some orientation dependence was also seen post mortem ([Kirilina 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7541072/)). A 4 s⁻¹ swing is about a third of the stria contrast.

This does not bias an orientation-based data term much, because it changes amplitude, not the band direction. It does bias the template and region term: band amplitude changes with folding, so it can mimic a facies change. **Put the angle θ between the cortical normal and B0 into the per-area template as a covariate.** Leaving it out would later need an orientation regression as a patch. **Inference:** the orientation effect should be largest where fibres are radial, because radial bundles in a bank or crown take a range of angles to B0 as the cortex folds. Cohen-Adad measured the largest effect in M1, and M1's laminar contrast is radial (next section). So in M1 θ is a first-order term of the template, not a correction.

**Not in the design.** The effect is judged overstated for these data. It is kept only as a future check (row 15, the θ covariate and T8 are not planned work), worth revisiting if apparent facies changes follow the folding rather than the areas.


**Present but invisible.** The OCT literature never models a layer that loses contrast while it still exists. Low-probability surfaces "stay smooth" because the prior carries them ([He 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7855873)). In cortex this case is the normal one, not the exception. Area 4 has no conspicuous tangential bands (its contrast is radial), area 18 shows only the inner band, and areas 17 and 9 are bistriate ([Aggarwal 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4262592/)).

**Inference.** The representation needs two separate per-column outputs: band thickness (dz/dT, which can be 0) and band contrast or confidence (which can be 0). If they are kept separate, an S1/M1 layer IV pinch-out and a stretch where a tangential band fades while the layer persists (for example the outer band in area 18) stay distinct, and the region term can use both. If they are not separated, the fit will drive thickness to zero wherever contrast vanishes, and a contrast mask will be needed as a patch.

**Boundary partial volume and the deep anchor.** Paquola and Bastiani both discarded the outermost depth samples. Wagstyl 2020's L6/WM boundary moved to the VIa/VIb gradient. Both point the same way. **Inference:** model the pia–fluid and GM–WM transitions as explicit mixed classes in the column likelihood, and let T = 0 sit at a contrast-defined deep transition rather than at an externally segmented white surface. The ex vivo T2* gap between WM (11 ms) and GM (26 ms) is large, so the R2* gradient is a good anchor. But it may not coincide with the histological layer VI/WM border.

## Where the literature conflicts with the plan

**The stria is at or below the target-band minimum.** The stria of Gennari is **about 250 µm** in silver stain, and the tangential track-density band is about 300 µm at 92 µm MRI ([Aggarwal 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4262592/)). That is **1.5–2 voxels at 160 µm**. Baillarger bands outside V1 are thinner or fainter; the notes found no numbers for them. The stria is still visible at this scale. Fukunaga resolved it at 150 µm. Sengupta's 200 µm multi-echo qT2* (TE 6.45–31.17 ms, close to the user's echoes) showed a superficial high-T2* band separated from the deep layers by the stria throughout V1 ([Sengupta 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5862655/)). At 100 µm, about 80% of the stria was visible under optimal conditions ([PubMed 15954140](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15954140) [U: authors unchecked]).

**Inference.** The structure tensor is sign-invariant, so a band thinner than the integration scale can still give coherent orientation from its two edges, at reduced amplitude. Wagstyl 2018 found an inflection (edge) more reliable than the thin L2 peak. Together these suggest restating the target as "a contrast **interface** pair coherent over at least 2–3 voxels", not as "a band at least 2–3 voxels thick". The superficial-versus-deep R2* step that brackets the stria is a wider target than the stria itself. Echo choice supports this. From Fukunaga's values, the relative stria contrast is about 12%, 24% and 34% at TE 9.37, 20.71 and 32.05 ms. CNR peaks near TE ≈ 30 ms, so the third echo carries most of the band signal (the calculation is in the notes and is not itself sourced).

**Radial-fibre contrast is laminar data.** Area 4 has no conspicuous tangential bands but a dense radial-fibre mid-zone with high FA ([Aggarwal 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4262592/)). M1 therefore has visible layers, and T there does not rest only on the boundaries and the prior.

**Not relevant at 160 µm.** Radial bundles do not give a different (linear) structure-tensor signature: a cross-echo structure tensor on M1S1 and V1V2 interior cortex gave median λ2/λ1 ≤ 0.20 everywhere, i.e. planar; no linear radial-texture signature at 160 µm. The orientation term treats all laminar structure as planar. The depth where the radial bundles fade is still a laminar landmark, seen as an ordinary edge parallel to the layers.

**Areal depth change versus smoothing scale.** Wagstyl 2018's 10 mm FWHM depth smoothing works only because equivolume L4 depth is nearly flat across curvature. It would blur the real L4 depth change at the V1 border. **Inference:** the lateral prior must allow band depth to change over about 1–3 mm with a kink, consistent with the no-fault rule. The notes estimate (unsourced) that the deviation from equivolume is about 0.1–0.3 mm, or 1–2 voxels. That sets a scale for the prior's strength.

**Dark is not always myelin.** Hypointensity in fixed cortex peaks at maximum myelin **and** at maximum cell density ([Fatterpekar 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12223371/)). QSM band sign reverses between V1 and temporal cortex ([Deistung 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.09.055)), whereas R1 and R2* fall monotonically from WM to pia ([Marques 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.12.009)). **Inference:** R2* is the safer depth feature. S0 is worth keeping as a separate channel for template identity, because cell-dense layers (including a doubled layer I at a seam) may show up there and not in R2*.

**Fused cortex.** The literature has no seam that is solved jointly with depth. DELFMAP (ex vivo, 0.25–0.35 mm) and CRUISE/ACE fix a skeleton or shock first ([DELFMAP](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/)). Two facts bear on the evidence ranking. Pial/L1 and L1/L2 are BigBrain's most accurate boundaries ([Wagstyl 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3000678)). Layer I was lost at 340 µm ([Bastiani 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5102896/)). **Inference:** a doubled layer I (0.4–0.6 mm, 2.5–4 voxels) is wider than a single layer I and could be detectable at 160 µm. That depends on whether layer I differs from layer II in R2* or S0 in fixed tissue, which the notes do not establish. BigBrain cortical thickness ranges from **1.8 mm (calcarine) to 4.5 mm (precentral)** ([Wagstyl 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3000678)). So the midline error |t_A − t_B|/2 can reach several hundred µm where a thick bank fuses with a thin one. The notes give no measured S1 thickness to make this exact for M1/S1.

**Unknown medium effect.** No source quantifies how Fomblin changes cortical R2* or laminar contrast. Sengupta used Fluorinert and Kirilina used PBS. Fixation lowers all relaxation times (T1 by up to 76% after 190 days) through cross-linking, not water loss ([Birkl 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/nbm.3477)).

## Conclusion

The literature does not lack constraints. It puts them in the wrong place. Every field rediscovered ordering, zero thickness and smoothness, and the mature ones (Li/Garvin/Song, Carass, He) moved them from post-processing into the representation or the energy, with measurable gains. The plan's z_c(T) design is the cortical version of that end state. What the literature adds is a short list of nuisances that are specific to cortex and ex vivo GRE, and that no transferred method handles: orientation to B0, invisible-but-present bands, partial volume at the boundaries, and a contrast-defined deep anchor. Each is cheap to put in the energy now and expensive to patch later.


The more consequential correction concerns targets. At 160 µm the stria and layer I sit at or below the 2–3 voxel threshold. The method should therefore fit interfaces and orientation rather than resolved bands, and T0 should be rescoped to say whether this works before the solver depends on it.
