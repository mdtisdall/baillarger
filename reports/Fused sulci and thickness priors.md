# Carry thickness as belief; place contacts as posteriors

No surveyed cortical tool places the contact between fused banks using what it expects each bank's thickness to be. FreeSurfer-style collision, DELFMAP and ACE skeletons, PBT distance maxima and DiReCT diffeomorphic fronts all resolve fused banks symmetrically, and they move off the midline only where some CSF intensity is left. None reports uncertainty for the contact. Geostatistics has the missing piece already built. NR's COHIBA builds a surface between two well-resolved reflectors twice, once stacking interval thicknesses down from the top and once stacking them up from the base, and then combines the two with weights that minimise variance. It also has a separate option that rescales interval thicknesses in proportion to fill the envelope. These are the two halves of the user's design: a Gaussian posterior for the contact, and normalized arrival. The Gaussian algebra shows when each holds. If the thickness variance grows in proportion to thickness, the posterior is exactly proportional sharing. That variance law is what a column built of independent volume increments gives, and it matches the user's principle: where contrast exists the data decide, and where it does not the model falls back to equal volume. A principled thickness field is a sparse Matérn/SPDE Gaussian Markov random field on each bank's WM surface. It needs a level term, mesh-aware weights and a joint solve with the contact. Its calibration has to be checked by masking visible tops, because no prior work reports calibrated thickness intervals. The idea of weakening lateral coupling where the laminar profile changes has a partition-level precedent (Schaefer's gradient-weighted MRF) but no thickness-field precedent in these notes. Facies-controlled thickness and attribute cokriging were not covered. The recommended next experiment is the Gaussian thickness field with constant coupling, a per-voxel contact posterior, and leave-out calibration, run before contrast-weighted coupling. The baseline is KellyKapowski with a thickness-prior image.

Status labels used below: **[verified]** means checked against a primary page during the research. **[unverified]** means from memory or a snippet. **[inference]** means derived here, with no source. Two project decisions taken since the research began are applied throughout. (1) The unifying principle: contrast decides where it exists, and equal volume is the fallback, in depth, laterally and at contacts. (2) Equivolume is the default background metric, and other background costs stay pluggable.

## Every surveyed tool splits fused banks symmetrically

The mechanisms differ, but their geometry does not. DELFMAP treats WM as a charged conductor and GM as a dielectric with GM-probability permittivity. Streamlines from opposite banks "collide inside sulci", and that skeleton becomes the barrier for the pial level set **[verified]** ([Osechinskiy & Kruggel 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/)). ACE builds its barrier from the skeleton of a CSF-weighted distance. Its skeletons have spurious branches, need a minimum-distance cutoff near WM, and CRUISE with default settings failed at 0.35 mm **[verified]** ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/)). MaACE adds multi-atlas evidence where CSF is invisible **[verified, abstract]** ([Huo et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4927397)). DiReCT's diffeomorphism cannot cross itself, so closed sulci become infinitesimal gaps. With no CSF, **the banks come out symmetric**, and fundus thickness is overestimated **[verified]** ([Das et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836782)). For FreeSurfer, self-intersection prevention is documented ([mris_make_surfaces](https://surfer.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/fswiki/mris_make_surfaces)). That fully fused banks therefore come to rest near the GM midline is **[unverified]**: no FreeSurfer document stating it was found, and the source (`mrisurf_deform`, `MRIScomputeBorderValues`) is the authority. In every case the contact is equidistant in some metric, so the user's M1/S1 result (+0.92 mm into the thin bank) is the predicted failure of the whole family, not of one tool.

Thickness priors in these tools are **caps, not expectations**. FreeSurfer reports thickness up to a 5 mm default ([mailing list](https://mail.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/archives/list/freesurfer@nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/thread/SMW6JU25IGDV2CAOZR5MG3YBTLIUVK4O/)). Whether that limit also bounds the pial search is unverified. DELFMAP uses d_max = 6 mm ([PMC3296314](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296314/)). DiReCT is the closest precedent. Its displacement stays below a user-defined, spatially varying τ(x), points at the limit get zero update, and its Fig. 8 shows a prior of 6 on one bank giving asymmetric bank thickness **[verified]** ([Das et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836782)). KellyKapowski exposes this as `--thickness-prior-image` ([man page](https://dyn.manpages.debian.org/unstable/ants/KellyKapowski.1.en.html)). A cap constrains a bank only when it binds, and only from above, so it cannot pull a thin bank's contact outward **[inference]**.

PBT is the caveat that matters for novelty. Its abstract says that projecting WM-distance maxima onto GM handles "sulcal asymmetries" without explicit skeletons ([Dahnke et al. 2013, via snippet](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/fumie.costen/pastwork/DATA/readingmaterial/corticalthickness4.pdf)). **The full text was not read.** Whether its maximum can sit off the geometric midline is the key open detail. PBT's projection carries thickness along the column, not laterally from neighbours where the pia is visible. None of the tools reports a posterior on the contact. The probabilistic parts of the tools are fuzzy tissue memberships used as forces, and no study validated contact position against histology in unequal fused banks. UNSURF adds a warning: ensemble and dropout variance correlated weakly or negatively with thickness error, while a discrepancy measured on the final surface worked ([Mehta et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00498v2)). Uncertainty must be computed on the quantity that is reported.

## COHIBA already splits an envelope between two stacks

Interval-thickness ("isochore") mapping models each interval as a trend plus a zero-mean Gaussian random field. Trend coefficients carry a Bayesian prior that stays "robust for any N, including N = 0" wells. It reduces to universal kriging as the prior widens and to simple kriging as it tightens **[verified]** ([COHIBA manual §20](https://projects.nr.no/sites/default/files/files/cohiba_manual_7_0.pdf); [Abrahamsen 1993](https://projects.nr.no/sites/default/files/files/Bayesian-Multilayer-Abrahamsen-1993.pdf)). Wells correspond to columns with a visible top. The kriging SD is near zero at them and rises to the prior SD beyond the variogram range. That is the propagation-with-uncertainty behaviour the user wants. Abrahamsen also states that uncertainty accumulates down a stack, because every interval above a surface adds its variance.

The direct analogue is COHIBA's **multiple models** (Abrahamsen 2005). The manual says, roughly, that when it is unclear whether to build from above or from below, COHIBA combines the alternatives so that precision is optimised **[verified]** ([manual §1.2, §11](https://projects.nr.no/sites/default/files/files/cohiba_manual_7_0.pdf)). In the Brent example, stacking from the top gives a base SD of about 29 m and depth conversion gives about 15 m, so the weights are 0.79 and 0.21. The combined SD is below both, and "intervals with large uncertainty are used for absorbing the inconsistencies". The weights are sensitive: doubling the base SDs moves its weight from 78.8% to 48.4%. Correlated models can give weights below 0 or above 1. A common warning reads "average thickness … is negative", which flags a conflict between models. Separately, `<scale-isochores-to-seismic-envelopes>` multiplies every isochore by envelope thickness divided by package thickness (eq. 17.2), which is **proportional rescaling**. `<weight-isochore-package-above>` is an explicit knob between trusting the stacks and trusting the envelope ([same](https://projects.nr.no/sites/default/files/files/cohiba_manual_7_0.pdf)). Inequalities, such as an ordered surface lying above a well path, are handled by kriging with inequality constraints through data augmentation, which gives truncated-Gaussian posteriors rather than clipped estimates **[verified]** ([Abrahamsen & Benth 2001](https://projects.nr.no/sites/default/files/files/MathGeol-33-Abrahamsen-Bent-2001.pdf)). Proportional stratigraphic layering, with pseudo-time 0 to 1 across a zone, is the reservoir name for normalized depth ([US 12596203](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12596203)).

**The contact posterior [inference, standard Gaussian algebra].** Along a path that crosses the fused gap, let D be the gap and h_A + h_B = D, with priors h_k ~ N(μ_k, σ_k²) carried from each bank. Then

E[h_A | D] = μ_A + σ_A²/(σ_A² + σ_B²) · r, with Var = σ_A²σ_B²/(σ_A² + σ_B²) and misfit r = D − μ_A − μ_B.

The bank with the larger variance absorbs most of the misfit. The sharing rule follows from how σ scales with thickness:

| Variance law | Share of misfit to A | M1/S1 (3.5 vs 2.3 mm) | Equals |
|---|---|---|---|
| σ² constant | ½ | 0.50 | equal split of the misfit |
| σ² ∝ μ (sum of independent increments) | μ_A/(μ_A+μ_B) | 0.60 | **proportional = normalized arrival** |
| σ² ∝ μ² (constant CV, as in Brent's 30%) | μ_A²/(μ_A²+μ_B²) | 0.70 | μ² sharing |

Proportional sharing is also exact when the two banks share one multiplicative scale error. Two consequences follow. First, the rules differ only in how they share r. Where r is small, all three agree, which explains how a single per-bank thickness worked along the M1S1 stretch. Second, once σ_k comes from a propagated field, it is dominated by the distance to evidence rather than by thickness. The posterior then leans toward the bank whose visible top is nearer, which proportional rescaling cannot do. The variance-∝-volume row is where decision (1) enters the contact. Under equivolume, each bank's coordinate counts volume per unit WM area, and the prior "variance proportional to the volume carried" makes the zero-evidence posterior *equal volume fraction*, i.e. normalized arrival in the default metric **[inference]**.

## The Gaussian field must be proper, mesh-aware and solved jointly

With quadratic ρ, E(t) is a GMRF. Precision Q = W + λL with W = diag(1/σ_c²), mean from Qμ = W t̂, and covariance Q⁻¹, whose marginals come from sparse Cholesky and selected inversion or from sampling **[inference]**. The rigorous version is the SPDE/FEM Matérn field on a triangulated manifold, with a sparse precision built from mass and stiffness matrices **[verified]** ([Lindgren, Rue & Lindström 2011](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/an-explicit-link-between-gaussian-fields-and-gaussian-markov-rand/)). The graph analogue is the graph Matérn GP ([Borovitskiy et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.15538)). The specific α = 2 precision τ²(κ⁴C + 2κ²G + GC⁻¹G) is **[unverified]**, quoted from memory. Five lessons carry over.

First, add a **level term** κ²I with a prior mean. L alone leaves the constant unpenalised, so a fused component with no evidence gets infinite variance. With the level term, variance saturates at a population SD, the analogue of COHIBA's Bayesian trend, and the prior mean can be area-specific once regions exist. Second, use **cotangent/FEM weights with a mass matrix**, so that λ is a physical correlation length rather than a property of mesh density. This matters near fundi at 160 µm. Third, prefer a **second-order** (α = 2) prior. First-order priors make "tents" whose variance grows linearly between evidence points, while second-order priors extrapolate gradients. Fourth, keep the core **Gaussian, not robust**. Huber, TV and Cauchy penalties lose the closed-form posterior. TV is not discretization-invariant ([Markkanen et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.06135)), and Cauchy increments need MCMC. If kinks are needed, an IRLS line process is cheap: conditional on its edge weights the field is Gaussian with covariance (W + λDᵀΩD)⁻¹, and the weights show which edges were cut. Fifth, **solve (t_A, t_B, contact) jointly**. Treating per-column marginals as independent and then smoothing the contact again counts smoothness twice and makes σ too small. The joint precision stays sparse **[inference]**.

Graded evidence needs care with weights. A faint dark line is evidence about the contact and therefore about the top. It must enter once, either as t̂ or as the contact likelihood ℓ(x), not as both. A landmark-predicted top carries a calibration error shared by the whole bank, so it acts as a bank-level random effect, not as independent per-column noise **[inference]**. The dark-line likelihood is often bimodal or flat. Evaluate p(x) ∝ N(x; x̂, σ²)·ℓ(x) exactly on each column's 1-D samples, as Rathke's chain factor does, and do not fit a Gaussian to it ([Rathke et al. 2014](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1403.8003)). Rathke is the main precedent for a full posterior over layer boundaries: a Gaussian shape factor alternated with discrete column chains by mean-field inference. It reports uncertainty as mutual information, not as thickness intervals. Song et al.'s soft shape and context priors give a global MAP by max-flow, from 6.30 to 5.14 µm error ([Song et al. 2013](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:23193309&resultType=core&format=json)). **Their equation form, convex pairwise penalties on the deviation from expected offsets, is from memory and unverified.** The full text was behind a reCAPTCHA. Calibrated intervals are therefore this project's own validation task. Mask visible-top columns in blocks, re-propagate, and check that z = (t_true − μ)/σ has SD ≈ 1 and that 95% intervals cover about 95%. Stratify by distance to evidence and by whether an areal border lies between. Report the per-column misfit r/√(σ_A² + σ_B² + σ_D²) as a model check.

Decision (1) simplifies the lateral model. If the propagated quantity is equivolume thickness, i.e. volume per unit WM area, the geometric curvature effect that Bok describes is absorbed into the metric. **No separate curvature trend is needed.** The open question is only a weak residual. Add a COHIBA-style trend coefficient on curvature with a prior centred at zero, and look at whether its posterior moves away from zero. A second simplification is metric-neutral **[inference]**. The contact score per voxel, log p(x) = −(T_A(x) − t_A)²/2σ_A² − (T_B(x) − t_B)²/2σ_B² + log ℓ(x), with each t and σ read at that bank's own column foot, needs no column pairing and no sum constraint. That matters because volume coordinates from two banks do not add to a shared "D". The closed-form sum formula above is its special case in the distance metric along a shared straight path. Keeping other background costs pluggable therefore costs nothing at the contact step. Only T and t must use the same metric.

## Contrast-guided coupling has partition precedents, not thickness ones

The notes hold precedents only at the partition level. Schaefer et al.'s gwMRF combines a global-similarity data term with a Potts term whose weight falls with the local gradient between neighbours. Cutting is cheap where fingerprints change, and closure comes from the labelling **[verified paper; energy form unverified]** ([Schaefer et al. 2018](https://people.csail.mit.edu/ythomas/publications/2018LocalGlobal-CerebCor.pdf)). Gordon et al. merge watershed basins when the median boundary strength on their shared border is low ([Gordon et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4677978)). Schleicher-style Mahalanobis or Hotelling's T² distances between blocks of laminar profiles found areal borders in MRI at 340 µm, at the precentral crown and fundus ([Bastiani et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2016.00487)). The non-stationary SPDE extensions allow a spatially varying range ([Lindgren, Bolin & Rue review](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.01084)). The notes do **not** contain a thickness field whose coupling is weighted by laminar-profile similarity. **Facies-controlled isochores and collocated cokriging of thickness with a seismic attribute were not searched**, and the seismic notes say so explicitly. Any claim about those literatures has to wait for a targeted search.

The design λ_cc' = λ0·g(D_cc') has one strong property **[inference]**. If D is computed before the thickness solve, the field stays a GMRF with exact covariance. This is option (d) in the probabilistic notes, deterministic down-weighting of border edges. Lowering λ across a border correctly widens σ on the far side, so a fused stretch cut off from its evidence by a border shows honest uncertainty. The level term's prior mean then dominates there, which argues for a per-region prior mean shared with the Potts labels. The profile distance must not depend on the unknown top. Compare profiles in absolute equivolume depth from WM over the deep part of the column, which the earlier report showed needs no pial surface. Equivolume also removes most curvature-driven profile change, so g sees fewer false borders on crowns and fundi. Make g continuous so that gradual borders couple partially. Area 3a sits in the fundus, often exactly in fused cortex, and the near-WM profile is still measurable there, which is the reason to use it. To avoid circularity, compute D from fixed near-WM features in the first pass and share borders with the Potts model only after both have converged once. The B0-orientation effect on R2* can mimic contrast changes. The earlier report judged it overstated, so keep it only as a check if apparent borders follow the folding.

## Next experiment: constant coupling first, then contrast-weighted

Run three things on the almost-touching M1S1 stretch, the mildly unequal M1S1 stretch and the similar V1V2 stretch, all in 3D, in the equivolume metric.

**Step 1.** Build each bank's thickness field as an SPDE GMRF on its WM surface: cotangent and mass weights, α = 2, a level term, evidence from visible-top columns with one weight class. Show maps of μ, σ and the leave-out z-scores.

**Step 2.** Compute the per-voxel contact posterior from T_A, T_B and the field. Show its mean and HPD band against the user's marked gap and against the constant-thickness normalized-arrival line. Show the misfit map r as well, and where |r| is large, show how the three sharing rules diverge.

**Step 3.** Only then add λ0·g(D) and check whether the field kinks at the 4/3a/3b transitions without degrading the z-scores.

The baseline is KellyKapowski with `--thickness-prior-image` set to μ (and to μ + 2σ). It tests whether a cap alone reproduces the gain. These are diagnostic views read by eye, not pass criteria.

## Conclusion

The literature changes what normalized arrival means. It is no longer a heuristic beating the midline: it is the posterior mean of a two-stack Gaussian model under a specific variance law, variance proportional to the volume carried, which is the equal-volume fallback stated at the contact. That makes the user's principle one model rather than three rules. Contrast-free depth, contrast-free lateral thickness and contrast-free contacts all reduce to equal volume, and evidence moves each one only as far as its precision earns. The real gain of the probabilistic version is not a better split where thickness is well known. It is the lean toward the better-observed bank, honest widening across borders, and a misfit statistic that says where the model is wrong. Two items gate any novelty claim: PBT's handling of "sulcal asymmetries" (full text unread), and the unsearched facies-controlled thickness and cokriging literature.
